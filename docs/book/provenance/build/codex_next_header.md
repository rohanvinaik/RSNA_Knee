READ THIS FIRST. It says what went wrong last time, and the rules for this read.

In your last read of this project's book (the "second fresh read"), you graded every chapter AGREE, AMEND or
DISAGREE by whether you would build it that way, and you ranked "amendments". Those amendments were then applied to
the founder's design as if they were warranted. They were not. They rested on your judgment of what works, and on
warrants the founder never gave. That was hallucinated authority, and the work it produced had to be withdrawn.

The facts you must hold for this read:
- **Nothing is built.** For the most part the code does not exist. There is no functional codebase that meets the
  design intent, and there is no evidence of anything: no measurement, no run, no result.
- **The design is the founder's.** He alone is the authority on what works and on what the design intends. Something
  that looks wrong to you may be critically important by his design.
- **A citation is not evidence.** The canon (THEORY_OF_THE_CASE, KNEE_ARCHITECTURE) says what the design
  specifies. It never shows that anything works.

What you may report:
1. **A provable error**, each with its proof:
   - logic or arithmetic;
   - an internal contradiction, quoting both passages exactly, with their locations;
   - an order of work that cannot execute, naming the dependency;
   - a checkable external fact, such as the competition's data or definitions, or a file format.
2. **A question for the founder.** Where his design is silent or ambiguous, say what is unresolved and why. Do not
   answer it.

What you may not do:
- propose a change to the design, a mechanism, a rule, a threshold, a role or an order of work on your own judgment;
- grade any chapter by whether you would build it that way;
- treat any design text, canon passage or citation as evidence that something works;
- say that anything is built, measured, validated, invariant or shown.

If you are not sure whether a point is a provable error or your opinion, it is your opinion. Put it as a question.
