**SURE: Claude’s amendment is mathematically defensible, with a missing assumption.**

For fixed λ, suppose

\[
z=\theta+\epsilon,\qquad E[\epsilon\mid\theta]=0,\qquad
E[\|\epsilon\|^2\mid\theta]=p.
\]

Then

\[
\widehat R(\lambda)=\lambda^2\|z\|^2+(1-2\lambda)p
\]

is unbiased for the squared-error risk of \((1-\lambda)z\). Gaussianity is unnecessary for this fixed linear estimator. Claude is right about that, and I narrow my earlier objection accordingly.

But a zero-mean **null** establishes \(p=\operatorname{tr}\Sigma_{\rm null}\) only under health. It does not establish that the same additive, zero-mean nuisance with the same trace persists under pathology, altered anatomy or measurement uncertainty. Estimated \(p\) also carries uncertainty. Finally, selecting λ from the observed \(z\) does not inherit the fixed-λ unbiased-risk guarantee unchanged.

**Joint recommendation:** keep this as a conditional fixed-shrinkage risk analogy, not an established SURE interpretation of KneeDiff or a James–Stein guarantee.
