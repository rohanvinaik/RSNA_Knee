## On KneeDiff = GenomeVault GDiff (NOT sparse-wiki)

> "The probability space collapse idea is actually not at all sparse-wiki related. I'm
> thinking entirely of GenomeVault for that. Specifically the 'GDiff' part. Call it
> 'KneeDiff' loool."

> "The idea from GenomeVault was to make the privacy information-theoretic by creating
> a 'platonic ideal'/reference genome by taking several public genomes and sort of
> squashing them together. I actually over-engineered it in GenomeVault in a way that
> makes it SUPER useful here — where the nucleotide at a position has genuine ambiguity
> in the reference genomes, the 'platonic'/reference genome actually encoded that
> position as a statistical superposition base-pair. Like i said — overengineered for
> genomic data, where the only possible input is a base pair, not a pixel of certain
> color and opacity, and with cross-pixel/neighbor similarity linking as a core part
> of what makes a picture more than a sequence of pixels. But here? That base design
> is VERY useful for building a multi-dimensional reference source."

> "we have our 'platonic' knee image that's a holographic projection of some standard
> reference ones, and we have the analysis image we need to process, we can just kind
> of line it up nicely so all the positions resolve cleanly (maybe we should find a way
> to deterministically flag key positions to help it line up right?), then literally
> subtract! The point isn't to really stare at the opacity of a pixel that's off on the
> side, literally not even part of the patient's knee or leg, so that should all just
> zero out. Then we have the diff, which is the actual signal we need to process!"

> "A full image has WAY too high a S/N ratio, it's flooded with pixels that mean
> nothing to the analysis, but the KneeDiff format eliminates 99% of the noise, and the
> HDC architecture is like the JSON format for reasoning to bring it to a classical
> architecture in an AI-readable format."

> On sparse-wiki's actual role: "Sparse-wiki is just a way to cleanly and
> deterministically encode and traverse data otherwise considered messy/'subjective'/
> 'semantic', etc."
