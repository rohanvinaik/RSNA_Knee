# Consult: `knee.lenses`

A note on epistemic status up front. What follows about MR physics (chemical shift, magic angle, fat-suppression failure, coil falloff, red marrow, slab averaging) I know with confidence; it is textbook and I have seen each of them ruin an intensity rule. What I say about this corpus's series composition, slice geometry, and FOV habits I am inferring from typical knee protocols and from what you've told me; I have not seen the data and I say so where it matters.

## 1. Point-wise sampling, and where interpolation is confined

Point-wise is the right call, and the key fact that makes it principled is that a 2D multi-slice knee series is not a lattice of points, it is a stack of **slabs**. A voxel in a 3.5 mm-thick slice is a measurement of the whole 3.5 mm slab, not of its centre plane. So when a sagittal place falls "between" two coronal slices, there are only two cases: it falls inside some coronal slab, in which case that coronal voxel *is a measurement of that place* and reading it is not interpolation at all; or it falls in the inter-slice gap, in which case the coronal acquisition genuinely did not measure that place and the row is silent. Slab membership is `|dot(p − c_j, n)| ≤ thickness/2` using SliceThickness (not SpacingBetweenSlices, which includes the gap) — deterministic, non-interpolating, and it produces silence exactly where the instrument was blind. That is the through-plane rule and I would state it as a law of the module.

Within the same orientation, check geometry equality first. Many protocols copy the slice prescription from one sagittal series to the next, so ImagePositionPatient, ImageOrientationPatient, PixelSpacing and the slice set agree to within a fraction of a voxel. When they do, the place maps to an integer index in both and there is no interpolation anywhere; record this as a provenance digit (grid-identical / slab-read / silent). That provenance row will matter later, because grid-identical conjunctions are stronger than slab-read ones and a decision should be allowed to know which it has.

In-plane, interpolation is unavoidable only if you insist on sub-pixel values, and I would not for digits. Bilinear blending at a cortex–marrow or meniscus–fluid edge manufactures an *intermediate* value from a dark and a bright neighbour, and "intermediate" is exactly the cartilage class in your table. So the digit should be read from the containing pixel (nearest), and bilinear should be confined to the profile readings — the gradient-type rows — where blending across the edge is the measurement you want and your SAME harness already admits it. That is the confinement: bilinear lives in profiles, never in a tissue digit.

Two things point-wise sampling silently assumes, and you must measure rather than assume. First, **the LPS frame is shared only if the patient did not move between series**. Knees in a coil are fairly stable, but 1–3 mm shifts between acquisitions are routine. You already have the machinery to measure this: B2 transports a bounded dark band along normals and reads the recipient's own plane at the same place. The signed offset between where the cortex was expected and where it was found, aggregated over an object, is a per-study-pair motion reading. Record it as an argument; widen the slab tolerance by it if a decision asks; do not silently correct the frame. Second, **slab averaging is anisotropic in a way the conjunction will feel**: a sagittal place 0.4 mm wide read by a coronal voxel is being compared against a 3.5 mm average along the sagittal in-plane direction. A meniscal rim or a cartilage layer thinner than that slab will read intermediate in the coronal lens even when it is pure in the sagittal one. The support shading should therefore not count a slab-read acquisition as equal to a grid-identical one when the object's in-plane extent along the foreign slab normal is under one slab thickness; that is a geometric fact about the object you can compute from the territory.

A dual formulation is worth keeping in reserve: instead of pushing places into the foreign series, pull the foreign voxels whose slabs contain the object's places and take their median per object. Same determinism, and it is cheaper when the object has many places and the foreign slab count is small.

## 2. Attacking the structural bootstrap

"Subcutaneous fat is the band just inside the leg boundary" fails in at least five places on a real knee, and the first of them is not a corner case, it is the dominant systematic error in the whole plan.

**Coil falloff.** The knee coil is a surface array; signal is strongest at the periphery and falls toward isocenter, often by 20–40 % across the joint. Subcutaneous fat sits precisely where the coil is brightest; marrow, Hoffa's fat pad and the intercondylar structures sit where it is dimmest. A single per-series middle anchored on peripheral fat will read central fat as "below fat" and central fluid as merely "at fat". This is the mechanism that killed your four intensity instruments wearing a new coat, because a per-series scalar is still an absolute level with respect to position. The remedy is structural and already within reach: you have three fat witnesses at three radii. Subcutaneous fat (the compartment touching air), **marrow** (the interior of the B1 dome, which is bounded by dark cortex and is fatty in the adult epiphysis), and Hoffa's fat pad (the compartment bounded anteriorly by the patellar tendon's dark band and posteriorly by the femoral trochlea — constructible as an interstice, which is your own architecture). Read each where it exists; their disagreement *is* the bias-field measurement, and every place should be stated against the nearest witness, with which witness recorded.

**The anterior knee.** Over the patella and patellar tendon the subcutaneous layer is a millimetre or two; "just inside the boundary" lands on the prepatellar bursa, the tendon or patellar cortex — dark on everything. A fixed band depth is wrong; the band must be bounded on its inner side structurally, by the first dark line inward (fascia), which is a watershed territory touching the air boundary, not an EDT shell.

**The frame edge.** Coronal and axial FOVs commonly cut the thigh and calf; sagittal ones sometimes cut posteriorly. Where the leg touches the image border there is no air boundary and "just inside" is arbitrary tissue. Your air-connectivity mask gives you this distinction for free: boundary-against-air versus boundary-against-frame. Only the former seeds a witness.

**Fat-suppression inhomogeneity.** On FS series the subcutaneous fat at the periphery, and especially posteriorly and far from isocenter, is frequently *not* suppressed. This does not break fat-as-position, but it breaks fat-as-dark-on-FS, which is the premise of two of your six classes. Turn it into a row: the fraction of the subcutaneous compartment that reads bright on the FS lens is a per-slice fat-sat-quality witness. Where it is high, every "dark(FS)" and "bright(FS)" digit in that neighbourhood is stated under a recorded defect.

**Muscle as "bulk by volume"** is the weakest witness. Through the joint line a sagittal stack is mostly bone, fat pads, ligament and cartilage; muscle is confined to the gastrocnemius heads and popliteus posteriorly and the distal quadriceps and hamstrings at the stack ends. I am inferring the proportions, but I would not trust that the largest non-bone compartment is muscle in every series or every slice. A structural muscle witness is a large compartment that touches neither air nor a bone interior, is bounded by fascia, and has a convex-ish cross section above some centimetres squared — with silence rather than a guess when no compartment qualifies on that slice.

**Fluid.** A distended recess is also a finding (effusion), so using it as the fluid witness entangles the witness with a label. Define the recess structurally — the suprapatellar interstice between the quadriceps fat pad and the prefemoral fat pad — and read whatever is in it; most knees have a few millimetres of physiological fluid there. But do not make fluid depend on a fluid witness. Your table already does not: fluid is above fat on FS, above muscle on non-FS T2, below muscle on T1. All three are statements against witnesses that exist in every knee.

## 3. The ternary band and the middle

`±k` flickers is the right *resolution* but the wrong *width* on its own, because the question "is this place at the middle" is not "is this voxel distinguishable from a number" but "is this voxel indistinguishable from the witness tissue", and the witness tissue has its own spread — bias field, partial volume, genuine heterogeneity. A fat compartment in VST units might span several sigma. So the band should be `k · sqrt(σ_place² + s_witness²)`, with σ from the noise law and `s_witness` the witness population's own measured spread (a robust one, MAD or interquartile, so a stray vessel does not widen it). Both terms are measured; `k` remains the one recorded argument. With SNR 20–40 on PD-FS, a bare ±1σ band would make "at the middle" nearly empty except for the witness itself, which is a degenerate ternary.

More important than the width: **there should not be one middle per series.** Your tissue table is written entirely as comparisons to named tissues — below fluid, above meniscus, dark relative to fat. So a row is a pair (acquisition, witness), and the middle of that row is that witness's median at that place's nearest witness instance. "Above fat on PD-FS", "at muscle on T1". The series-wide median of the leg is dominated by whichever tissue is largest and drifts with body habitus; it is comparable to nothing. A per-slice witness median tracks the bias field along the stack; fall back to the series median of the witness when a slice has too few witness places, and record which (local / series / absent) as provenance.

One caution on flicker units across studies: the VST makes one flicker one sigma, which makes *distinguishability* comparable, but the magnitude "fat is 18 flickers above muscle" is a contrast-to-noise ratio and depends on field strength and protocol. If you ever want a magnitude beside the digit, express it as a fraction of the fat–muscle gap in that series — dimensionless, and the classical way to make knee intensities comparable. The digit itself stays ternary.

## 4. The knee's row layout

Scales: the place (voxel); a neighbourhood (16×16 in-plane is roughly 6 mm, which is sensible); the object (territory on one slice); the object's stack (the same dome across adjacent slices — knee objects are per-slice, and their continuity through the stack is a real scale the sibling did not have); and the compartment or plate (the whole bone, the whole joint).

Lenses, as you say, are acquisitions — but **the lens label is itself a measurement**. Derive it from EchoTime, RepetitionTime, InversionTime, ScanOptions and the sequence name, not from SeriesDescription, and keep PD-FS, T2-FS and STIR as distinct lenses: cartilage is bright on PD-FS and intermediate on T2-FS, and STIR suppresses by T1, so it kills things fat-sat does not. Localizers and duplicates must be excluded structurally (slice count, FOV, orientation).

Per (object, lens) the readings I would carry: the digit against fat; against muscle; against fluid where a recess witness exists; a gradient row (edge strength in flickers from the admitted profile, the analogue of Gradient); a split row — is this territory one tissue or two in this lens, read as prominence in the territory's own intensity histogram, the exact analogue of Split Quality and essential for a meniscus with a bright cleft inside it; a dark-share row — fraction of places below the muscle witness; and a composite, the conjunction class. Relational: the compass neighbours' classes and the ring, the territory's boundary band read in every lens, because cortex that reads bright on FS in a segment is a finding on the ring, not the interior. Provenance: lenses present; geometry relation per lens pair; witness source; motion offset; fat-sat quality.

Add one geometric row the thesis does not have but the knee needs: the orientation of an elongated dark object's long axis to the scanner B0 (the LPS S axis). See §5.

## 5. Where a conjunction is confidently wrong

Four cases, in roughly the order I expect them to hit you.

**Failed fat suppression without a T1 lens.** If a study has sagittal PD-FS and non-FS T2 but no T1 (I don't know this corpus's mix, but many knee protocols are exactly that), fluid is "bright(FS) and bright(T2)". Unsuppressed fat satisfies both. Posterior subcutaneous fat, posterior Hoffa, and anything near metal will be called fluid with full concurrence. Metal makes it worse: susceptibility voids read dark on everything, which is ligament.

**Residual red marrow.** Hematopoietic marrow in the distal femoral and proximal tibial metaphysis — common in young, athletic and female patients — is darker than fat on T1 and brighter on FS. That is your marrow-edema conjunction, inside a bone, satisfied by normal anatomy, patchy, often symmetric. Intensity cannot separate it; the only classical handle is that red marrow is metaphyseal and spares the epiphysis and subchondral plate, while edema that matters is subchondral and focal. That is a relation to the physeal scar and the articular surface, i.e. to objects, which is at least the right kind of question for your architecture.

**Magic angle.** Collagen oriented near 55° to B0 reads intermediate on short-TE lenses (PD, T1) and dark on T2. The curved PCL, the popliteus tendon and the patellar tendon's insertion do this routinely. "Intermediate on PD, dark on T2" is simultaneously the normal magic-angle signature and the signature of mucoid degeneration. This is why the B0-angle row belongs in the bundle: a fibre's angle to the S axis is computable from the territory's principal axis in LPS, and it is a witness that exonerates.

**Chemical shift and partial volume at interfaces.** Along the frequency-encode direction, fat–water boundaries carry a one-to-two pixel bright band on one side and dark band on the other; across 3–4 mm slabs, meniscus, cartilage and fluid blend into intermediate. Both manufacture the cartilage class at precisely the interstice where a cartilage defect decision will be made. Chemical shift direction is readable from the DICOM phase-encoding axis; partial volume is the geometric support caveat from §1.

## 6. Compute shape and the cache boundary

Per study, roughly five series of 25–40 slices at 512² is about 40 M voxels. VST, witness medians, digit assignment, dark share and `bincount`-style per-object aggregation are all NumPy. The cross-lens mapping is one affine per series pair applied to arrays of places, followed by slab membership and fancy indexing — NumPy, and fast. The profile sampler is already a Numba twin; the only other Numba candidates are per-object histogram prominence over many small territories and anything that loops per place with branching. Nothing here justifies C or Rust; the module is memory- and I/O-bound, not arithmetic-bound. Use `cache=True` on the Numba kernels so JIT cost is paid once per kernel session.

The budget question is the hidden set size, which I do not know. At even 10 s per study, 2 000 studies is five and a half hours, which is inside nine but not comfortably once a Kaggle box is slower than your laptop. Measure the per-study wall time on the 58 and treat it as a hard argument.

Cache in three keyed layers. Per series, keyed by SeriesInstanceUID (or a hash of pixel data plus geometry): the noise law, the VST volume or its parameters, the LPS affine, the lens label, the leg mask, witness masks and per-slice witness medians. Per study pair of series: the geometry relation, the slab maps, the motion offset. Per object, keyed by (series, slice, object id, build-version hash): the rows. Nothing survives a code-version change without that hash in the key. On Kaggle with internet off the cache is within-run memoisation plus whatever you precompute on train and attach as a dataset; hold one study in memory at a time and write rows out columnar.

## 7. What you are not asking

You are not asking how the lens axis gets its labels; that is a measurement with its own failure modes and it deserves a provenance row. You are not asking about motion, and point-wise sampling is silently betting on its absence. You are not asking about the bias field, which I think is the single largest threat to any per-series middle and is why multiple structural fat witnesses at different radii matter more than any choice of `k`.

You are not asking whether per-object digits are enough for the findings. Several of the twelve are sub-object: a meniscal tear is a bright line inside a dark object reaching its articular surface; an ACL tear is a discontinuity along a fibre; these need place-scale digits retained *within* the object with their positions, not just the object's aggregate and shade. The bundle should keep the place rows addressable, not merely summarised.

You are not asking what laterality does to your frame conventions and to the B0 angle when the knee is left versus right, or what happens when a study is actually a bilateral or a post-operative knee with hardware.

And one that belongs to decisions rather than lenses, but will arrive: macro ROC-AUC wants a rank, and "abstain" is not a rank. The lens does not care, but the architecture must have an answer for where an abstention sits in the ordering before the first decision is built on these rows.
