**1. Is the refusal correct?**
Conclusion (twelve abstentions today) holds on the stated facts, but one of its grounds is wrong and one fact is overlooked.

- Overlooked: Fracture and Contusion are specified on *a* bone, indefinite. Ownership already yields integer-indexed T1 extents. Whether an indefinite subject may be satisfied by ranging over every T1 extent (coherence read per object, no kind needed) is exactly the founder's "break in the structural coherence of a 'bone' object" — and ownership *has* computed those objects. Your claim "no resolved fact selects *any* subject" is therefore stronger than the facts support for findings 0–1. Whether that existential reading is permitted is his ruling (see 4), not a reason to assert nothing is selectable.
- The T2/T3/T4 subjects: correct that none exists — the relations stage returns `construction_absent`, so there is no interstice, span or envelope to select. That is the clean ground.

**2. Tier naming.**
"Object / interstice of a facing pair / span / envelope" are structural, not anatomical; no smuggling. But the tier is not a selector: three findings (2, 3, 4) would each abstain with the identical phrase "a T2 interstice". The spec says the abstention "names the missing subject"; whether a tier that three subjects share counts as naming *the* subject is the founder's to rule. Note also that a future selector among T2 interstices will need the side sign; a field whose *value* is "medial"/"lateral" would violate the schema rule, a signed integer relative to a declared frame would not — decide which before writing it.

**3. Provable errors.**

- *"nothing orders the 597 pairs."* False. The pairing stage reports pairs **per sampled profile row**; a row index is an ordering, and the sample has a position in the plane. The pairs are ordered geometrically; what is missing is the sign that says which end of that order is medial, not an order.
- *"The fact that would select it is femur/tibia identity plus the lateral sign."* The first half contradicts the founder verbatim: "we don't need to be able to link numbered objects to anatomical roles to do this." Asserting identity as the required selector reframes the thesis in the ground of your abstention even though the abstention itself stands. The missing facts on the stated record are `construction_absent` and the lateral sign — neither is a kind-label.
