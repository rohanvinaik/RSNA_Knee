## §4 Collapse (2113–2307)

**2115–2119.** "The first move of **H1** (vision built from the laws) is to stop rereading what
the movie's laws hold nearly constant (§3). We call this move **the collapse**. … The collapse is
the first instance of the squint (§1.3, §4.5)."

**2134–2149 (§4.2).** "A lens (§1.1) is here such a measure, and there are four, partly
orthogonal … The four are co-equal, and each often sees what the others miss (Figure 14, panel A;
Figure 10, panel B). Their agreement and their disagreement are both information (§1.2)."

**2151–2154.** "Each lens stands at its own sharpest slice, and its picture is the recorded light
at that slice. There it draws the **bodies** it sees: contiguous regions that stand out in its
picture. … A lens with nothing clear to say abstains. Nothing is averaged or merged."

**2156–2160.** "Two lenses at about one depth read one plane two ways, and each corrects the
other's borders. Lenses at different depths are the three-dimensional structure itself."

**2167–2169.** "The figure's first row marks where each picture is domed: where the light at a
place outshines its surround at a nucleus's scale, a bright center over a darker surround, as over
a nucleus. The commit reads that shape one way only, for a cell (§5.5). The dome of §6.2 is a
different thing: the territory of places that one bright center holds."

**2192–2195 (§4.3).** "The composite finds signal across depth that the four lenses miss. It is
also the least clean of the five pictures, so it never pulls a cell and never decides alone: it
points. Where it holds light that the lenses' pictures do not, at what §5.6 defines as a site, the
lenses that can speak there are asked (the site search)."

**2197–2199.** "Depth is peeled, never reconstructed. Each cell taken leaves the volume through
its band, the slices it occupies, together with its halo, and the next round reads again what is
left."

**2247–2252 (§4.5 The Squint).** "Each of these moves makes the signal less detailed on purpose,
because the questions asked of it are categorical: whether there is a shape here or only blur,
whether a region is one cell or two. Such a question needs a reading only to fall on the right side
of a line. For it, a reduced reading is cheaper and cleaner at once, and signal-to-noise rises as
the work falls, for the reasons §1.3 sets out. … The laws say which reductions are safe: those that
flatten only what the laws hold nearly constant, the precondition's third clause (§2.3, §3.6)."

**2254–2257.** "Figure 12 makes the squint literal. Reduced to the median light of each 16 × 16
square, a crowded plate still shows where its cells mass and where its ground is dark, as a

**2274–2276.** "Waltz found that precise distinctions produced his unique labels (Waltz, 1972),

none pooled into one score."

**2280–2283 (§4.6 What One Step Removes, and What Is Kept).** "The savings compound, and nothing

**2285–2289.** "Every loss is taken in a view or in how a reading is written, never in what is

## §5 The Structure: Stages 0 to 4, and the Loop That Empties the Box (2309–2640)

**2311–2315.** "A time point is resolved when the system can say three things of every place in
its volume: whether a cell is there, what shape it has, and that it is one cell and not noise. Most
of this the structure settles from the laws alone. It does so by elimination under law, the third
move of **H1**. Each stage removes only what a law says is not a cell, or takes out whole what the
laws say is one. What is left is read again, until **the box**, the whole three-dimensional volume
of the time point, holds nothing cell-shaped."

**2325–2330 (§5.1).** "Stage 0 sets out what every later stage reads: the four lenses' flat
pictures, each at the depth its lens chose, and the composite. Beside them stand cheap readings,
which we call **the natural philosopher's toolkit**. Each is made once, in milliseconds, where its
input first exists, and is kept for any stage to consult. These readings point, and never decide
alone. A coarse view of the whole field is itself a squint: it settles whether a plate is dense or
sparse, still or moving, more cleanly and more cheaply than a fine one. It narrows where the
expensive work runs, and its reading of the drift routes how the cells are followed."

**2332–2336.** "The readings of the whole field, taken once for the movie, are the **priors**: its
density, its distribution (even, spiky or one-sided), its profile down the depth, and whether and
how the plate drifts. Their words form the **regime key** under which a case from this field is
kept. Each round takes the same readings again over each lens's picture, and over a 16 × 16 grid of
squares laid on it, and keeps them."

**2340–2342 (§5.2 The Black).** "**Stage 1, the black**, removes from each lens's picture what is
knowably not signal. It reasons from the camera's dark and the haze (§3.1), and by them black is
local, never one number."

**2344–2351.** "Two filters act in order of seniority, a rule of authority (§1.11). The senior
filter removes every place with no light above the camera's dark, calibrated for each time point
and depth. It is narrow and knowably correct. It passes over much faint light, but where it fires
it is right, and there it decides alone. Where it keeps a place, the junior filter removes what does
not stand above the local haze. … it refines only what the senior keeps."

**2353–2357.** "Stage 1 deletes nothing. Its removals shape the bodies that one lens draws on one
slice, and the voxels stay for every other lens and every later round. That is what the principle
that there is no noise means here (§1.2): a removal is a decision by a reading entitled to make it,
kept as a record, never a discard. The same holds of light that belongs to no cell, which an expert
calls noise: to resolve light so, with confidence, is itself a finding (§1.2)."

**2361–2365 (§5.3 The Bodies).** "**Stage 2, the bodies**, reasons from the gestalt of an
<object>: contiguous, closed, and of a size a nucleus can have. It takes the places stage 1 keeps
whose light stands above their local base, the mean light of the kept places around them, and
joins them into contiguous bodies. It removes the specks, bodies narrower than 2 µm, since nothing
that small is a nucleus, and it stitches back fragments of one cell."

**2373–2383 (§5.4 The Lenses Speak).** "In **stage 3, the lenses speak**, and truth is assigned by
**concurrence**: assigned where separate readings agree, refused where they disagree, never by one
tuned pipeline or one voice. The readings are separate in that each lens measures one thing and
knows no question, so no reading wins by being brighter (§1.1, §1.11). The lenses meet by depth
alone. … 1. Where their bodies agree, each corrects the other's border; 2. Where a partner draws two
bodies where a lens holds one, that parting is carried forward to after the take; 3. A lens alone
at its depth keeps every body, since each lens often sees what the others miss; 4. Where two
disagree, neither overrules the other. The result is a cleaner set of each lens's own bodies, never
a merged picture: cells are pulled from the lenses."

**2408–2411 (§5.5 Commit and Pull-Out).** "**Stage 4, commit and pull-out**, takes out each cell
the views agree on, together with the light it spreads. It reasons from the optics of a known cell
(§1.8, §3.1). Read alone, faint light beside a bright cell could pass for a dim cell. Whose light is
it? Once the bright cell is known, the optics say the light is the bright cell's own, so the take
corrects for what a known cell did to its surroundings."

**2413–2418.** "The commit is the take's first check. It consults each reading of one counted cell
that can speak, apart from the others, and pools none of them into one score. … Where the readings
concur, the cell is taken, and the concurring readings are recorded. Where they disagree or
abstain, the cell stays for the next round, which reads it from scratch."

**2427–2430.** "The optics give the take a second check, which needs no annotation: once a cell is
out, its place should look like its surroundings. … Every object taken is a **candidate** until it
is called."

**2434–2437 (§5.6 The Loop).** "What a round leaves is the **residual**: the volume after the cells
found so far have been pulled out with their halos. By the Wayfinder thesis (§1.9), it is not
noise. It is the same kind of problem with added context, and the structure reads it again. Its
signal-to-noise is higher for the removal, since what stood hidden beside a bright cell stands out
once that cell is gone."

**2454–2458.** "Taking out the first round's cells also changes what the rest of this plate means,
by the Monty Hall move (§1.5). The residual inherits the principle, not the game's numbers. Its
removal is lawful: cells known to be cells, taken with their light. So objects in the residual that
look alike mean different things, given what was removed around them. The system keeps that
meaning. Each candidate carries how it came out (§6.6), and the clean-up reads it in the residual
(§10.2)."

**2460–2468 (the site search).** "Two targeted searches on the residual close each round. **The
site search** follows the composite, which points and never pulls. A **site** is a bounded patch
where the composite of the residual still shows light that no lens holds: stage 1 keeps its places,
no lens drew a body there, its light stands above its local base, and it is wider than a speck
(Figure 13, panel C). The search asks there, in a small block of the volume through every slice,
and only the lenses that can say something speak. A lens speaks only at a local peak of its own
focus that reaches a set height, and there it draws its bodies at its clearest slice. A lens with no
such peak abstains. … Where none speaks, nothing is taken."

**2482–2485 (reaching down).** "**Reaching down** reads the column of each cell just taken, on
what is left, for a second hump at another depth. … The taken cell's halo has already gone with it,
in the plane and in depth, so a hump that remains is not its spread light. The lenses that can speak
there are run there, and each object found is taken through its own band, its column read in turn."

**2487–2489.** "The next round runs stage 0 again on the residual, with every lens recomputed.
Each lens stands at the sharpest slice of the residual it has not used, so a later round reads
depths that the first round's cells hid. A lens with nothing left to say abstains."

**2493–2500 (§5.7 The Last Look).** "The loop ends when a round takes nothing. A cap on the number
of rounds is a reported safety, never the stopping rule. … **The last look** finds them by
structure, never by one brightness number, since a bright signal need not be cell-shaped. … Each
object still standing is read once, at its highest point, on the untouched light: by its hump down
the column, its dome (§6.2), its outline pulled taut, and its source, the taken cell it may have
come from."

**2635–2640 (§5.8).** "Every step obeys decide or abstain (§1.14). It answers only under warrant,
by its own contract, or says that it cannot. Each abstention is kept, with its light left for the
next round, and principled abstention of this kind is one of the strengths of a law-based design.
So the kept record of findings is whole. It is what one time point hands on: every taken cell with
its footprint, band and depth, and how it came out; every candidate; every decision with what was
known before it; and the residual. The language of §7 is written from that record, and the
clean-up and the agencies take up what the structure leaves."

**Figure 19 (B), 2563–2577, the round as the thesis draws it:** "STAGE 0 The flat pictures, each
lens at the sharpest depth it has not used; the composite; the cheap readings · STAGE 1 The black:
The senior filter (the camera's dark), then the junior (the local haze) · STAGE 2 The bodies:
Specks removed, fragments of one cell stitched · STAGE 3 The lenses speak: A cleaner view for each
lens; every cell counted once · STAGE 4 Commit and pull-out: Each agreed cell taken through its
band, with its halo · THE RESIDUAL, READ AGAIN: The site search, where the composite points;
reaching down".

## §6 Borders: The Data Topology of an <Object> (2642–2838)

**2644–2648.** "Every stage of the loop has drawn borders: the bodies of stage 2, the outlines by
which the lenses read each other, the footprint and band of every take, the halo that leaves with
its cell. Each says where one <object> stops and the next begins, and each is read from the data's
own shape. … An <object> is a region the structure can enclose, and each border it draws is a law
written as geometry."

**2650–2655.** "A nucleus shows itself by a gestalt. It is contiguous and closed, roughly round,
brightest at its center, several slices deep, and it holds a space of its own. Each of those words
comes from a law … Read as a relief of light, a nucleus is a peak. Its border is where the peak
falls to its surroundings, and a valley is where one <object> ends and the next begins."

**2664–2669 (§6.1).** "A body's **outline** answers the paradox. It is the body's border pulled
taut: the trace made as short as it can be while the body's area and topology are kept. … The
knowledge that a nucleus is roundish is thus applied as a pull, never imposed. No border is known
more finely than the flicker, so none is moved farther than its flicker."

**2699–2703 (§6.2 The Dome and Its Territories).** "A nucleus is brightest at its center, by the
optics of a fluorescent source, so the structure gives every place on a slice to a bright center.
A center's **dome** is the territory of places it holds, and a place's **dome link** is its
direction to that center. The centers are the peaks of the negative Laplacian of a Gaussian blur
at a nucleus's scale (Marr and Hildreth, 1980), a response that is positive exactly where a place
outshines its surround. Nothing is cut on strength, so a bright nucleus never silences a dim one.
Each place goes to its nearest center in the plane."

**2705–2711.** "The dome link says which places belong together. It is a cheap prior, drawn by
distance alone, and it never decides alone: where it errs is information (§1.2). … Two centers
alone do not make two cells, nor does one center make one cell. Where the dome link and a call
disagree, the disagreement is evidence, kept beside the call. Whether a candidate is one nucleus,
and where to part it, is read in part from whether its places point to one center or two."

**2725–2737 (§6.3).** "Two <objects> never occupy one space, and two at different depths are
different <objects> (§3.3). … the column decides, and a lens standing where the column holds no
hump abstains. A cell's footprint is the union of its bodies, pulled taut once more. … Two
candidates that share voxels are one object to settle, never two cells by rule."

**2741–2742 (§6.4 The Column and the Band).** "A nucleus is several slices deep, and down its
column its light rises and falls once, in one hump: its band (§5.5). A dip followed by a rise is
the valley before another cell."

**2760–2765 (§6.5 The Halo).** "In the plane, at each slice of the band, the take follows the
cell's drop-off without assuming its curve. It grows outward from the footprint, ring by ring, and
takes each neighboring place whose light is at most one flicker above the lowest light on the way
to it: light still falling away from the cell. It stops where the light turns up toward another
source or sinks into the haze, and it never enters another cell's footprint or a voxel already
taken."

**2812–2823 (§6.6 The Candidate Pool).** "Every take yields a candidate, an <object> pulled out
and not yet called. It carries what the structure knows of it: 1. Its footprint, its band, and its
column before the take; 2. Its own light before the take … 3. How it came out: its route (the first
pass, the site search, reaching down or the last look), its round, the lenses that held it, the
bodies over or under it, and whether its two depth readings agree. How it came out is a physical
fact … The pool is generous by design. The structure offers every region it can enclose, and the
expert's calls and the memory settle which are cells (§8 to §10)."

## §7.3–7.7 Silence and Black; Full, Sparse and Cheap; the Bundle; One Comparison (2978–3106)

**2980–2982 (§7.3).** "A reading made at its middle is the digit 0, written in its row like any
other digit. A reading never made leaves its bank silent, and a silent bank is the **informational
zero**, "no reading here", which makes no claim (§1.12). It is left out of every comparison, of dot
products and norms alike, so it never counts for or against anything."

**2984–2990.** "Black is silence in the full analysis. Where a lens's stage 1/2 clears a place,
that lens spends no full analysis there, and where stage 0's composite holds a place black, no
dome link or ring is written. Yet a black place still writes something. At an anchor it writes its
**sparse** vector in place of the full one: its cheap readings and, where the last look read
something there, the last look's. … A cell that every lens's stage 1/2 cleared can still be found
by the search for missed cells (§11.1)."

**2997–2998, 3006–3008 (§7.4).** "The heavy analysis runs at three time points, and the vectors
follow it. The full vector is written where that analysis ran, and the cheap vector wherever a raw
picture exists. Like is compared only with like. … The full vector is written from values the
rounds already computed, so writing it is only counting."

**3010–3017.** "Full and cheap vectors share bank names but read different sources. A full vector's
cheap-named banks hold the processed state … A cheap vector's banks hold the raw picture. At an
anchor each cell has both profiles … Set side by side, per context, they are **the calibration**."

**3041–3043 (§7.5).** "An <object>'s profile is the bundle of its places. One arithmetic serves
every scale, because a bundle of bundles is a bundle."

**3061–3065 (§7.6).** "A question reads the banks of the readings it is about, at any scale. Which
banks those are is named before the run it judges, in the **layout** … No bank carries a weight,
and none is chosen because it separates confirmed cases."

**3093–3099 (§7.7).** "No single number means anything here by itself: what it means is set by the
structure that reads it (§1.10). … So the representation decides nothing. It makes the structure's
readings and the memory's cases one kind of object, and meaning is read by comparing a profile
with confirmed cases, over the banks of a question."

**3101–3105.** "The bridge under H2 is measured, never assumed: that the profiles keep the
distinctions each question needs (§2.4). Beside it, and measured the same way, is how far the
views' errors are independent. A view's agreement counts for what it says beyond the others, so
their errors must be independent enough for their agreement to be evidence (§1.3, §7.2). An exact
code does not make an observation lossless. What an earlier black silenced, no profile brings back,
and that is why the readings behind each such decision are kept (§1.2)."

## §13.6–13.7 Work in Proportion to Change; Organization (4294–4341)

**4296–4297.** "The system computes a value once, and from then on measures only the change, the
smaller number (§1.4). Cells mostly sit, and the system mirrors them (memento mori, §3.2). Four
mechanisms carry this:"

**4298–4302.** "Compute once, keep, consult. A value is made once where it becomes known, and
handed to every consumer. A **kept value** is keyed by its producer, scope, camera reading and
encoding, each versioned, and by the state in which each slice it read last changed. It stays valid
while none of those slices changes. Each reuse enters the kept record of findings as the fact that
nothing changed there, which is itself information (§1.5), and each drop names its cause."

**4306–4308.** "Physics and abstention prune. Two cells cannot occupy one space, so neither the look
nor the search for missed cells computes anything at a place a cell already holds. A lens that
cannot speak at a depth is not computed there."

**4315–4321 (§13.7).** "Being organized about what is computed, where it is handed on and where it
is used is the substance of these mechanics. A run kept so shows what every answer rested on, and
who used every finding. Functions are of three kinds, kept apart. A measurement returns a number or
a field, with its scope and provenance. A decision returns an answer or an abstention, with the
evidence it rested on, as a named word and never a bare true or false (§1.12). An orchestration
only moves values between stages. Each function is small and pure, and does one thing a biologist
could name, such as reading the haze of a slice."

**4323–4326.** "Every categorical finding is kept as data where it is made: who made it, from which
state and inputs, whether it answered, abstained or found a disagreement, and why. Each use is kept
beside it with its consumer, so a finding never used shows itself, and the graph of what is used
where can be drawn from the record."

**4332–4335.** "The impure edge is thin: reading a movie, writing the output, drawing pictures. A
picture is a pure function from the run's values to an image array. The run asks for no picture and
imports no drawing code, so pictures are a layer that can be switched off."

**4337–4340.** "These mechanics ground the paper's claims of exactness and of cost. … The look pays
only for the cells, windows and rows it reads (§13.3), and a kept value is made again only where
something it read has changed (§13.6)."

## §1 — the information theory beneath the funnel (166–377, 465–601, 1042–1107, 1160–1251)

**193–197 (§1.2).** "**Unprocessed information** is the part that more structure, or a measurement
not yet taken, would remove: uncertainty that remains because reasoning has not been done or a
reading not made. **Randomness** is the part that nothing removes."

**235–241.** "In a closed, lawful domain, nothing measured is noise. We call this principle
**there is no noise**. "Noise" names the limit of a model, not a property of the world. … To
resolve a reading confidently to its cause is itself information, so information is reconstructed
from apparent noise, never thrown away."

**243–251.** "A disagreement between correct readings is information too. Each is right about what
it measures, so their disagreement says something about what they measured (§1.11). And one
identified thing can matter to two questions at once. In an image of a bone, the edema around a
fresh break is evidence for one question, whether a line that looks like a fracture is an active
one. For another, where exactly the fracture runs, it is signal to take out of view."

**253–260.** "Processing can lose information about a source, and never gain it. … the
data-processing inequality … A reduction can therefore keep everything one question needs. What
it drops is gone from it, and can be had again only from somewhere that still holds it. This is
the formal reason the record is never discarded. Every view is a reduction made for a question,
and the record it was made from stays whole for every other question and every later step."

**299–306 (§1.3 Choosing What to See).** "We call this move **the squint**: a view's fidelity
reduced on purpose, so that the dimension a question reads stands clear of the variation it does
not read. That less can answer better is literally true, and it is mathematics. Let a reading
spread over $N$ dimensions, with a signal $s$ lying in the $k$ of them that a question reads, and
independent variation of power $\sigma^2$ in every dimension. Projecting the reading onto those
$k$ dimensions keeps all of the signal and only $k$ of the $N$ shares of variation … so the ratio
of signal to variation rises by a factor of $N/k$. Removing from a view the components orthogonal
to the query removes the variation that lies in them."

**308–313.** "Pooling is the commonest case, with $k = 1$. If $n$ places share one level $s$, each
with independent variation … the signal-to-noise ratio of the amplitude rises by $\sqrt{n}$, and
that of the power by $n$. Blur is pooling in space. What a question does not read is noise only
for that question: for another it may be the signal, and the record keeps it whole for that one."

**315–325.** "A squinted view is lossless for what it measures. A tile's median is exactly that
tile's median, with no error in it. The view is lossy only against the full-fidelity signal it was
made from … Nor does the squint add information … What it changes is where the information sits.
Gathered into fewer readings, each with a higher signal-to-noise ratio, the information can be read
by a simple categorical test, and so the squint answers more cleanly and with less work at once."

**327–335.** "Rate–distortion theory makes the squint's economy exact … A categorical question
accepts any distortion that leaves its answer unchanged. … an exact answer needs $R(0) = H(A)$
bits, at most $\log_2$ of the number of answers. A question with three answers needs at most
$\log_2 3 \approx 1.585$ bits, which is what one balanced-ternary digit can carry (§1.2)."

**354–364.** "A properly designed lens measures one fact of the problem and is never wrong about
it (§1.1). It is a projection of the <object> onto the one dimension it reads. No view is the
truth alone, and what an <object> is, is what the views recover together. … $I(X; Y_1, Y_2) =
I(X; Y_1) + I(X; Y_2 \mid Y_1) \ge I(X; Y_1)$. This is the chain rule for mutual information …
zero only when the second view says nothing about $X$ that the first has not already said."

**467–470 (§1.5 What the Rest of the Problem Tells Us).** "Taken alone, an empty square of a Sudoku
could hold any of nine digits … It is never taken alone. … No uncertainty is left, though nothing
in the square itself was read. Its answer came from outside it, carried by the rules that tie the
squares together."

**488–500.** "Write $H_0(X)$ for the uncertainty of a model that admits every state alike, $\log_2$
of their number. A model that holds the law admits for $X$ only the states the law allows, a set
$A$. … $H_0(X) - H(X \mid S) = \big[H_0(X) - H(X)\big] + I(X;S)$. The first term is what the laws
remove by narrowing the admissible states, and the second is what the rest of the problem says
through them. … This is the sense in which a problem's structure is information."

**514–523.** "Constraint propagation turns this fact into computation. A constraint removes from
one part's possibilities those that no possibility of a neighbor can accompany, and each removal
can force others beyond it. From the junctions at each line's ends, Waltz's filter left a single
label on every line of his first scenes, with work that grew only linearly in the number of lines
(Waltz, 1972). … A map of $n$ regions has $4^n$ assignments of four colors, $2n$ bits of
possibility before any constraint. Coloring the map of the contiguous United States by depth-first
search, in an order arranged to be bad, would by Winston's estimate take longer than the age of the
universe. Reduce, as each region is colored, the colors still open to its neighbors, propagate each
reduction, and it takes seconds (Winston, 2010)."

**525–531.** "What something is not tells what another thing is. … Carnap and Bar-Hillel made
exclusion the measure of what a statement says. In their theory of semantic information, the
content of a statement is the set of possible states of affairs it excludes … A rejection, "not
X", is warranted by the structure of the problem, not by a reading that happens to line up with
the truth on one path through it."

**575–580.** "What a reading reveals about one part it reveals, through the laws, about the rest,
so a reading of another object bears on this one. To set that reading aside as noise is to say
that opening one door tells nothing about the others."

**1050–1055 (§1.9 The Residual).** "**The Wayfinder thesis** … states the general move. In a
structured domain, remove the plain part of the problem, classified structurally, together with
everything its laws say it caused. What remains is the same kind of problem with added context:
fewer and more ambiguous <objects>, with the strong signal that masked them gone, and more
structured than before, because what was taken is now known. It is not random noise. Read it again
by the same means. The removal discards nothing."

**1057–1074.** "$I(X_1, X_2; Y) = I(X_1; Y) + I(X_2; Y \mid X_1)$. Nothing is lost by peeling.
Taking the strong source first, then the weak one with the strong one removed, recovers all that
$Y$ says about both. … Let the strong source arrive with power $100N$ and the weak one with $3N$. A
decoder that has not yet resolved the strong source can only count it as noise … the weak source
carries, for each use of the channel, … $\approx 0.02$ bits. Read once the strong source has been
decoded and subtracted, it carries … 1 bit, nearly fifty times as much."

**1076–1082.** "This is superposition coding with successive decoding … In practice it is
successive interference cancellation: decode the strongest signal, rebuild it, subtract it, and
decode the next (Verdú, 1998). Radio astronomy found the same loop for pictures. Högbom's CLEAN
finds the brightest point of a map, subtracts there the instrument's response to a point source …
and searches the residual again (Högbom, 1974). That is nearly step for step how the structure
takes out a cell with its halo (§5.5, §6.5)."

**1177–1189 (§1.11 Authority, Agreement and Dissent).** "A wide lens holds every candidate a
question is about, and extras. What it holds settles nothing … What it does not hold can be
cleared outright, by a decision kept as a record, never a discard (§1.2, §5.2). This is reading by
exclusion … A narrow lens speaks rarely, and is knowably correct where it speaks, so there it
settles. Its silence settles nothing … We call such a lens **first among equals** … The senior
filter of stage 1 is a narrow reading of this kind, on whether a place is black. It fires only
where a place holds no light above the camera's dark … There it is right, and it decides alone:
the place is black, and so knowably not signal (§5.2)."

**1214–1218.** "Seniority orders two lenses that read one question in turn. The senior, weak but
knowably correct where it speaks, has full authority there. The junior, the stronger reading,
refines only where the senior abstains, and never overrules it. … The order is one of authority,
not of strength."

**1220–1227.** "Correct views can disagree, and then the disagreement is information. … A later
image makes them the three legs of one stool, each a true authority and none the whole. … A
disagreement says most between views that usually agree, since the rarer an event, the more it
says (§1.6)."

**1235–1250.** "A committee of views decides positively, negatively, ambiguously, or by a majority
with its dissent recorded, and for each ruling it needs a contract, written before it sits. … The
contract looks downward. … Truth is assigned by concurrence, and refused on disagreement (§5.4,
§5.5). The structure's word "one cell" for a candidate needs at least two families of readings for
it and none against, so one family against defeats it (§9.1). A family is the readings of one
source. They are not independent of one another, so they count once, and no evidence is counted
twice (§1.6)."
