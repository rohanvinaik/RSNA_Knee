### 1. Does the three-property reading capture his bar?

Mapping his sentence onto the three properties: *object / object* → symbols exist; *this signal* → a language exists; *means this label* → reasoning composes. Three of his five terms are covered. The two not obviously covered are **"vs"** (the pairing) and **"this expected signal"**.

"Expected signal" is the more serious one. ### 2. Generativity under existential evidence

**Established:** acceptance is one study; his ruling is "if it works properly once, then it works period," justified by "378+1213 is the same symbolic process as any other addition."

The lead's criterion as phrased — "a lens that needs re-tuning per study is brittle" — is a counterfactual about a second study. One run cannot observe it. **Stated plainly: as phrased, it is not checkable from one study and should not be presented as an acceptance condition.**

But the founder's addition analogy shows what *is* checkable, and it is the same thing restated: generality of a symbolic procedure is read off the *procedure*, not off repeated trials. You confirm long addition by inspecting that no step depends on the particular digits, then watching it run once. The analogue: **every threshold in a lens has a provenance that is either the series' own distribution or a named design constant, and none was set by looking at this study.** That is a property of the code and the record, inspectable in one run, and it is exactly what "edges read from that series' own distribution" already promises. Secondary, weaker within-run evidence: one study carries several acquisitions, and the digit is per acquisition — if the unchanged lens rule produces a readable digit map on each series, that is more than one execution of the correspondence, though not a second study.

What remains genuinely unobservable from one study is whether the *correspondence* is right elsewhere — whether "dome" picks out cells in the next dish. The founder has ruled that question out of scope by fiat ("works period"), and also out of this bar ("doesn't have to be good"). So the lead's criterion is a sound design principle for later lens work and should be moved there; as an acceptance condition it should be replaced with the provenance check.

### 3. The finite grading loop

**Established:** Keller got letter→mouth-shape for every letter and pair, then synthesised new words without being graded on each; the founder's sentence is the grammar: *object vs object, signal vs expected → label*.

The "words" here are the twelve predicates and their expected readings per pair. The "letters" are whatever the predicates are composed from without further hand correspondence. Reading the ten operations as a dependency structure: ops 9 and 10 compose op 6; op 6 composes ops 4 and 3 (a facing pair of parsed objects plus the digit read across them); op 7 is a bookkeeping consequence of op 4; op 5 refines op 4; op 2 parameterises op 3 but produces nothing he would grade visually.

So the smallest atomic set I can derive is two correspondences, with a third that is a construction rule rather than a reading:

- **The digit alphabet (op 3):** that −1 / 0 / +1 / silence per acquisition reads the signal as he would read it. This is the letter→mouth-shape analogue exactly: a finite alphabet, graded once per acquisition type, after which every "signal vs expected" clause is synthesised.
- **The boundary primitive (op 4):** that what the parse calls a measured boundary is a boundary he would draw. This is the "dome" — the single hand-made rule that makes `<object>`s exist at all.
- **The interstice construction (op 6):** not a reading but a rule — "between facing objects, the interstice is here." It needs one confirmation, because everything relational depends on it, but it is confirmable only after the first two are.

**What to put in front of him first:** the three-colour digit map with silence drawn as silence (op 3), one per acquisition, and the parse with measured versus constructed boundary distinguished (op 4). Not the predicates. If he confirms those two, he can write every predicate's *e* himself without grading twelve outputs, which is the Keller property. Showing predicates first would be grading words before letters.

**Inferred, cannot fully determine:** I don't know whether op 4's parse consumes op 3's digits or runs on the stabilised field directly. I also note the "expected reading *e*" from Q1 is not an atom to be *confirmed* but the thing he *supplies* after the atoms are confirmed — the loop's second half.

### 4. Identity as destination

**Established:** "anatomical roles let us design the logic… but they are not relevant to the actual process of a pipeline in action"; numbered regions and anatomical roles are "basically the same thing" to him.

**Op 5, "refined given its structural role":** the sub-terms listed — supported connectivity, constrained shortening, closure standing per chain — are topological and geometric. If "role" means the census from op 4 (what bounds this object, what lies across it, whether its chain closes), then role is a *relational* fact the pipeline resolved and no identity is assumed. If "role" is assigned from a kind-label (bone / fluid / cartilage) and the refinement rule is selected by that label, then identity is a destination reached before op 5 can run. **The text supports the first reading but does not exclude the second; I cannot determine it.** The test is mechanical: what is the argument that selects the refinement rule, and is it a value some earlier stage computed under property 1?

**Op 7, "owned by one `<object>`":** this is a partition, not an identification. Every place belongs to a numbered object or is named unassigned. Nothing here requires knowing what the object *is*. I find no identity assumption in op 7, and the residual map is the right rendering for it.

**Where I do find identity leaking in, which the lead did not ask about:**

- **Op 8, "the subject kind it was keyed on recorded."** Banks keyed on a *kind* of subject is the closest thing in the ten operations to a classification step. If "kind" is anatomical, this is identity-as-destination in all but name. If "kind" is structural (closed chain vs open, bounded-by-N), it is fine. Cannot determine.

The founder's answer is that numbered position *is* the role, so a subject chosen by structural position (the object across from the largest closed chain, say) is licensed.
