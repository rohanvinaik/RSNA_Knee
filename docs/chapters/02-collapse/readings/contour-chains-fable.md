## Q1 — Freezing the constructed arcs

**From the text.**

- The sibling's band is the location uncertainty of a *reading*: "how far a traced border point may
  move — the flicker of the light there over how steeply the light falls across the border"; its
  warrant is "No border is known more finely than the flicker, so none is moved farther than its
  flicker"; its range is clamped to [half a place, half a speck].
- The knee's census distinguishes a supported place ("a measured dark reading") from a constructed
  one ("a decision of the watershed, not a reading").
- "A missing reading is silence, never a negative."

So the sibling's "unread → free to the ceiling" is a rule about a border whose *location* was not
measured precisely. In the cell world every mask border is a border of something, so "within half a
speck of here" is a true statement about it. A constructed arc is not an unread border; it is a
non-reading — nothing about the object's surface was measured there, and "how far may it move" has no
referent. Hence:

- band = ceiling asserts "a border, located to within half a speck" — false by the census's own type;
- band = 0 asserts "a border, located exactly" — also false by the census's own type;
- band = 0 lies outside the sibling's declared range [half a place, half a speck]. It is not a value
  the sibling admits but a new mode; whether `pulled_taut`'s termination or its staircase removal
  relies on the floor is **undetermined**.

The lead frames this as "inverting the sibling's rule for one case". From the text, no inversion is
needed: the rule's domain (unread readings of real borders) does not contain the constructed arc at
all.

**Mine.** The lead's conclusion — the pull has no licence to move a constructed arc — follows (the
pull's licence is a reading; there is none). The encoding "band zero, on the loop" is a different
statement with two provable side effects for as long as the arc stays in the loop the pull iterates:

1. *Neighbours' chords.* A supported place next to a frozen place is pulled halfway onto a chord one
   end of which is a watershed decision. Bounded by its small band, but the junction geometry is set
   by a reading and a decision together.
2. *The area-hold.* The text's sequence is: ease the whole loop along its normal to hold the traced
   area, then clamp every point back into its band. A frozen point is clamped back; the area
   correction is therefore carried entirely by the supported places, up to their bands. At 0.88–0.94
   supported (coronal) that transfer is small. At 0.24 supported (sagittal) the 76% of the loop that
   is decision dictates where the 24% that is measurement sits, and "the area it was traced with" is
   three-quarters watershed. The hold then pushes convex supported arcs to the outer edge of their
   bands, systematically.

*The case freezing hides.* Not a case where a divide should move *within a band* — I find none in the
text; a divide is not located by flicker and slope, so no band-shaped movement is ever warranted for
it. What it hides are two cases where the divide should be *replaced*, by a different operation with
its own measurement:

- (a) A divide between two fragments of one object, or between bone and non-bone. The same knee gives
  six territories in one series and nineteen in another; if those are counts in comparable frames
  (per slice or per series is **undetermined**), not both are counts of bones, so some divides
  separate things other than two bones. Such a divide should dissolve (merge), not move. Its
  measurement is the relief, and Figure 21 C/D says two centres are evidence, never a decision.
  Freezing enshrines the decision.
- (b) A divide where the true cortex is displaced from the EDT pass — the divide runs between two
  domes through tissue, and each bone's real surface lies to one side of it, possibly by more than
  half a speck. What belongs there is completion from the flanking supported arcs (Q2), not a
  movement of the divide; the divide's position is irrelevant to where the cortex is.

Freezing-on-the-loop makes both look resolved: the arc has a position, is rendered in the same stroke
as a supported arc, and can be consumed by a predicate as a surface.

*Third option, in the text's own vocabulary (mine).* The constructed arc has **no band** — Absent, not
0, not ceiling — and is not in the pull's domain. The pull runs over the supported arcs as open
chains, each under its measured bands; the divide is carried separately as what the census already
says it is — a relation between two territories, typed constructed, with its relief. This is the
"silence" encoding: an Absent band cannot be clamped to, added, or drawn as a width. Costs, also from
the text: the oval and the area-hold are defined on a *closed* loop's area and second moments and are
undefined on an open chain — which coincides with Q3's question of whether they should be dropped;
and a chain's two endpoints have one neighbour each, so the chord step is undefined there — the
endpoint rule is **undetermined** and must be a contract decision.

**Undetermined.** Whether `pulled_taut` terminates with a zero band; whether the knee's loop carries a
per-place band *type* (the sibling's band is a scalar); what the knee's ceiling is.

## Q2 — The completion bar

**From the text.**

- "No bridging of non-touching gaps exists anywhere in that code." The sibling has no completion
  operation.
- "A hole narrower than its band closes, a wider ring hole stays" is pinned beside "two lobes keep
  their neck and leave the waist open". I read "hole" as a ring's void (given "ring hole"): an inner
  loop collapsed by per-point freedom. The waist is a bay, and it *stays open* when wider than the
  band. Neither is a gap in an outline being bridged.
- So completion is not the sibling's hole-closing. The nearest analogue is the one the earlier reader
  found: a constructed stretch at band = ceiling under the chord step is driven, iteration by
  iteration, toward the straight chord between its two supported anchors — bounded by half a speck of
  *where the watershed traced it*. That is (i) a straight-line completion, not a continuation of the
  flanks' curvature; (ii) anchored to the divide's own position, which is irrelevant to where the
  cortex is (Q1 b); (iii) exactly the "maximal freedom where nothing was measured" regime. And if
  Q1's freeze is adopted this consequence vanishes: nothing in the existing operation then completes
  anything. **Completion is a new operation for the knee, by the text, and it is in tension with
  Q1**: the rule that yields the sibling-style completion is the rule Q1 removes.

**Mine — the shape of the bar.** The bar as stated fuses two licences that answer two different
questions:

- (i) *Could the instrument have missed a boundary here?* — why absence of a reading is uninformative.
  Inputs: voxel, slab, and the cortex's geometry relative to them.
- (ii) *Is the completion determined by the flanking data?* — why drawing is completion and not
  discovery (the founder: "ENOUGH points … pattern-completion … not wild discovery"). Inputs: the
  sampling spacing of supported places and the agreement of the flanks.

Both are needed. A gap can pass (i) and fail (ii) — short, but its flanks diverge (a mouth) — or pass
(ii) and fail (i). The form "L shorter than one scale S" cannot express a conjunction.

*Provable limit of (i) as stated.* A length derived from voxel size and slab thickness does not bound
the in-plane length over which a cortex can be missed:

- Through-plane: a surface at angle φ to the slice plane traverses one slab of thickness T over an
  in-plane length T·cot φ, unbounded as φ → 0. The slab bounds the smear *distance*, not the miss
  *length*.
- In-plane: a cortex thinner than the in-plane voxel is partial-volumed along its whole length,
  however long.

So voxel/slab bound the miss-length only for a cortex steep to the slice and thicker than a voxel. The
bar must either measure the surface's angle (from through-plane neighbours or 3D tangents of the
flanks) or confine itself to (ii) and state the orientation assumption. Whether the knee's loop is 2D
per slice or 3D is **undetermined**; marching squares and "loop" read as 2D.

*What else breaks it (mine), each with what must be measured.*

- A real opening narrower than S — a notch mouth, a joint space edge-on. Measured defence: the
  tangents of the two flanking arcs at the span's ends and the miss-distance of their extrapolations
  at the span's midpoint, compared with the local band; divergent flanks refuse with a code,
  convergent flanks complete.
- Speckle. The brief reads as if the "scattered speckle outside the leg" is supported-typed. If so, a
  span between a cortical arc and a speckle "arc" is completable by length alone. Measured defence:
  the run length (contiguous supported places) of each flank against a declared minimum — the
  sibling's "loops under four points are dropped" is the analogue.
- Completion through bright tissue. A constructed span means no dark reading was found; a completed
  cortex crossing tissue brighter than its flanks by more than the flicker is contradicted by the
  image. Measured check: intensity along the completed path against the flanks' readings and the
  local flicker.
- Through-plane corroboration. Whether the span's in-plane location is supported in slices z±1 is the
  strongest form of (i). Whether the build looks is **undetermined**.
- Long-span silence read as "open". A span that fails the bar keeps its prior type and a refusal code;
  it is never evidence that no boundary exists ("silence, never a negative").

*One measured candidate for S itself (mine, with caveat).* The inter-bead gaps *within* each flanking
arc are the instrument's demonstrated miss-lengths on this very cortex under this very acquisition; a
span no longer than a gap the arc already spans is a miss of the observed kind. Caveat: defining
"within an arc" by a gap threshold is circular unless the arc is defined otherwise, and a maximum is
outlier-sensitive.

## Q3 — The admissible shape prior

**From the text.**

- The oval is "set by the loop's own area and second moments" — a global second-order shape model —
  warranted in the thesis by "the knowledge that a nucleus is roundish … applied as a pull". The
  earlier reader established that roundish is a nucleus's physics and that radial growth fails for the
  patella, the condyles and the osteophyte. The oval's warrant does not transfer; the text determines
  that.
- The chord step is local. I checked the earlier reader's cos²(κ/2): under p ← p/2 + (p₋ + p₊)/4 a
  mode e^{iκn} is multiplied by (1 + cos κ)/2 = cos²(κ/2). A straight stretch is a fixed point
  ("straight where straight"); high-curvature violations decay geometrically.

**Mine — a provable property that decides more than taste.** The chord step followed by the clamp is
projected gradient descent. With E = Σ|p_{i+1} − p_i|², ∂E/∂p_i = 4(p_i − m_i) where m_i is the
neighbours' midpoint, so the halfway move is a gradient step of 1/8 on E, and clamping each point into
its band disk is projection onto a product of disks. E is convex; the disks are convex and compact.
Therefore:

- for any rate α ∈ (0, 1) in place of the ½, the iteration converges to a minimiser of E over the band
  disks, and the minimiser set does not depend on α — the unmeasured ½ is a speed, not a shape
  parameter, and the output can be *stated* as a contract: "the loop of least summed squared segment
  length that lies inside every band, centred on the trace", unique up to degenerate ties (a rigid
  motion feasible within every band at once);
- the area-hold (a quadratic equality) and the oval (a pull toward a moment ellipse) are not convex;
  with either present the limit depends on the rate and on the order of the three moves, and the
  output can only be stated as "what the procedure converged to".

So "chord step alone, under a per-point band" is the one configuration of the sibling's moves whose
output is a well-posed deterministic object independent of the unmeasured constant. That argument is
from the mechanism, not from anatomy.

*Its bias, also provable.* Points on a convex arc of radius R at angular spacing θ move inward by
R(1 − cos θ)/2 per step until their band stops them; a straight stretch does not move; a concave arc
moves outward (its chord lies outside). Chord-alone therefore ends with convex measured arcs at the
inner edge of their bands and concave ones at the outer edge. With the area-hold kept and some places
frozen or tightly banded, the compensation lands on the free places and flips the direction (Q1, point
2). Neither bias exceeds a band — the thesis's limit holds — but its *direction* is a property of the
configuration, and a relation between two pulled surfaces (a width read as the distance between them)
inherits up to two bands of offset in a known direction. The contract must declare it (Q6).

*Area-hold, kept or dropped:* **undetermined** as a choice. What the text determines is its warrant —
"the area it was traced with" is a measured quantity exactly to the extent the loop is supported. At
0.88–0.94 it mostly is; at 0.24 it is mostly a watershed decision. The supported fraction is already
measured. Whether it gates the hold or the hold is dropped is the founder's.

*"If not that, what form":* I find nothing in the text that the chord step under a measured band does
not already express; the local, band-bounded minimiser *is* "straight where straight, low-variance
curve where curved" with geometric decay of violations. The one thing it does not express is a
*direction* of growth ("push outward radially"); the earlier reader showed the axis that would carry
it is circular, and I have nothing to add.

## Q5 — Ordering and the variance-stabilising composition

**From the text.** The band is flicker over slope — an intensity over an intensity-per-place —
dimensionally a length in *any* intensity units, raw or stabilised. The earlier reader's "no physical
width because the transform is not composed" needs qualifying.

**Mine — the proof.** For a smooth monotone transform g, to first order at a place of intensity I,
flicker′ = g′(I)·flicker and slope′ = g′(I)·slope, so flicker′/slope′ = flicker/slope. The composition
does not confer a width the raw ratio lacks. What it does is make *one global* noise figure valid at
every intensity. So the raw band is a measured allowance if, and only if, the flicker in it is *read
at the place* ("the flicker of the light there", as the sibling defines it). If the knee's flicker is
one global raw number, the band is wrong place by place by the ratio of the true local noise to that
number — and the first-order argument is weakest exactly where g′ changes over the span of one
flicker, i.e. where the noise is most signal-dependent; the text does not say where that is for this
instrument. Which of the two the knee build does is **undetermined**, and the earlier reader's claim
turns on it.

*For the contract.* It can honestly promise "movement bounded by a measured allowance" now, provided
it names the flicker's provenance as one of two admissible kinds — local at the place, or global after
stabilisation — and admits no third. The composition is a prerequisite of *passing* that contract only
in the global case; it is not a prerequisite of writing it. Two further items the promise depends on,
independent of the transform: (1) the knee's ceiling — "half a speck" has no knee value in the text;
without a declared ceiling in physical units an unread band is unbounded and the promise is empty;
(2) the slope's direction — "how steeply the light falls across the border" is a derivative along the
profile; a profile cast off the normal underestimates the slope by the cosine of its angle and
over-grants the band, and the band is then an allowance *along that profile*, not an isotropic disk.
Whether the knee's band is isotropic is **undetermined**.

## Q6 — What must be forbidden

Each: the failure, then what the contract says so it cannot happen — a type, a post-condition or a
code, not a norm. **Mine** throughout, built on the text's three laws (named codes; silence not
negative; none moved beyond its flicker).

1. *A measured place moved beyond its measured band.* Output places carry (traced position, band,
   flicker and slope provenance); the clamp is the last move; a post-condition asserts
   |p − p_traced| ≤ band for every supported place; the band is computed from a read flicker and a
   read *falling* slope only.
2. *A decision treated as a reading.* The place type {supported, constructed, completed} is carried
   through every stage and into rendering; the band is a sum type Measured(width) | Absent, so an
   Absent band cannot be clamped to, added, or drawn; the pull's domain is typed to supported places;
   a predicate consuming a surface position takes the type and returns a code naming its dependence
   when any input is constructed or completed.
3. *Freedom where nothing was read.* The sibling's "unread → ceiling" is not imported: in the knee an
   unread flicker, an unread slope or a non-falling slope yields Absent, never a width. The text
   determines the conflict between that rule and "silence, never a negative"; the resolution is mine.
4. *Completion that invents.* Completion is a function returning a named code — COMPLETED,
   REFUSED_LENGTH, REFUSED_DIVERGENT_FLANKS, REFUSED_FLANK_TOO_SHORT, REFUSED_CONTRADICTED_BY_IMAGE,
   REFUSED_UNRESOLVED_ORIENTATION — and only COMPLETED produces places, every one typed completed and
   carrying the identity of its two flanks. A refused span keeps its prior type and is never read as
   "open".
5. *A global shape imposed.* The update at a place takes (previous, self, next, band) and nothing
   else; no term reads the loop's area, moments or centroid without a declared, measured warrant. The
   oval is excluded by the signature, not by advice.
6. *Area held on an unmeasured area.* Either the hold is absent, or its warrant is stated as the
   supported fraction of the loop and refused below a bar written before the run; it is never applied
   to a loop part of which is frozen, since the frozen part's area is then carried by the measured
   places (Q1).
7. *Topology changed by smoothing.* The pull preserves the number of territories and of places; merge
   and split are separate decisions carrying the relief as evidence, each with its own code; a loop
   shrinking below the sibling's four points is a refusal, not a deletion.
8. *Smoothed-off detail discarded.* The output is (pulled loop, per-place displacement from the
   trace), never the pulled loop alone — the knee's form of "what a body drew outside its outline is
   kept as a finding".
9. *Rate- and order-dependence passed off as a result.* The output is defined as the minimiser of a
   stated problem (Q3), so SAME between reference and twin is a statement about a solution, not a
   trajectory; if non-convex terms are kept, the rate and the order of moves are part of the contract
   and of SAME.
10. *Undeclared systematic bias inherited by relations.* The direction and magnitude bound of the
    pull's bias per configuration (Q3) is written in the contract; any relation between two pulled
    surfaces carries it as a declared offset bound.
11. *A name outrunning its warrant.* "Supported" means "a dark reading was found on the profile", not
    "cortex found"; the brief uses both phrasings, and if the speckle outside the leg is
    supported-typed the two provably differ. The contract names the reading; "cortex" is assigned only
    where a further warrant is measured.
12. *A constant tuned.* The ceiling, the completion scale, the flank minimum and any rate are each
    derived by a stated formula from named measured inputs (voxel size, slab thickness, supported
    spacing, flicker) and written before the run; a test reads them from the data, never from a
    hand-set value.
