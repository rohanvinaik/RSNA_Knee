# Answer to the consult (Fable)

I take the frame as given. Nothing below proposes learning. Where I say "known," it is textbook knee anatomy or MRI physics; where I say "inferred," it is my inference about this project's data or pipeline, which I cannot see.

## 1. Anatomical errors in the predicate table

**Fracture — predicate too narrow.** "Break in structural coherence of outline" is right for displaced fractures and avulsions, but a large share of fractures scored on knee MRI are non-displaced or occult: tibial plateau fractures seen as a dark line in trabecular bone with surrounding marrow edema, subchondral insufficiency fractures (a dark line parallel to and beneath an intact subchondral plate), and stress fractures. In all of these the *outline* is intact. The predicate needs a second clause: a linear low-signal structure inside the marrow, dark on every sequence, with edema around it. Avulsions (Segond fragment at the lateral tibial rim; tibial spine avulsion of the ACL; patellar sleeve) are a third form: an extra small bone object adjacent to a matching defect. Distractors that satisfy "break in outline" and are not fractures: the physeal scar (a dark transverse line across the metaphysis in anyone young, and visible for years after), a bipartite patella (a separate superolateral ossicle with its own cortex), and bone islands. These must be resolved as objects so they can be excluded.

**Contusion — subject right, predicate non-unique.** "Interior signal anomaly, boundary intact" is satisfied by contusion, but also by subchondral bone marrow edema of OA, insufficiency fracture, osteonecrosis, and red (hematopoietic) marrow. Red marrow in the distal femoral metaphysis is normal in young adults and women; it is intermediate on T1, mildly bright on fat-suppressed sequences, roughly symmetric, and spares the epiphysis. The founder's "recall at diagnosis" is the right place to disambiguate, but the *parse* must give it the inputs: location of the edema relative to named sub-regions (lateral femoral condyle at the sulcus terminalis plus posterolateral tibial plateau is the pivot-shift pattern that co-occurs with ACL tear; anterior femur plus anterior tibia is hyperextension; and so on).

**OA rows — "gap narrowed" is a radiograph concept imported into MRI.** On a non-weight-bearing supine MRI the bone-to-bone gap is nearly uninformative. What MRI shows directly is the *cartilage*: two hyaline layers, one on each subchondral plate, each with a measurable thickness profile. The "walls" of the interstice are therefore not bone; they are cartilage layers backed by subchondral plates. The OA predicate should be: cartilage thickness profile with focal or diffuse loss (including full-thickness areas where the dark subchondral plate faces the interstice with nothing in front of it), plus marginal osteophytes (marrow-containing outgrowth continuous with the cortex beyond the normal condylar contour), plus subchondral change (edema, cysts as fluid-class pockets inside bone, sclerosis as dark marrow). For the patellofemoral row, note that at the near-extension used for scanning the patella sits on the proximal trochlea, so the *contact* region is small; the subject is the whole patellar facets and whole trochlear cartilage, not just what is in contact.

**Meniscus — "continuity" misses most tears.** The radiologic criteria for a meniscal tear are (a) abnormal intrasubstance signal that unequivocally contacts the superior or inferior articular surface of the meniscus, ideally on two adjacent slices, or (b) abnormal morphology — blunting, truncation, a displaced fragment, extrusion. Most positives are (a): a line inside an otherwise continuous dark wedge. "Continuity" catches bucket-handles and radial tears through the free edge, which are a minority. Also, the meniscus is not merely "content of the interstice": it is attached — the medial meniscus to the deep MCL and capsule along its periphery, both menisci to the tibia at their roots. Root tears and meniscocapsular (ramp) lesions are attachment failures, not body failures.

**ACL — the intercondylar interstice is not fluid-filled.** The cruciates are intracapsular but extrasynovial. The notch contains two ligaments, fat, and synovial reflections, not joint fluid. This matters for how the interstice's "fill" is characterized. The predicate should also include signal (edema inside the band on fat-suppressed series) and course (an ACL that lies flatter than the notch roof, the Blumensaat line, is torn even if a band is visible). Chronic tears can show a continuous-looking scarred band in the wrong orientation.

**MCL — grade matters.** MCL injury is graded 1 (edema around an intact ligament), 2 (partial), 3 (complete). Grade 1 is probably the most common positive and has intact continuity. The predicate must include periligamentous and intraligamentous edema and thickening. Also, the MCL is not cleanly extra-articular: the superficial layer is, but the deep layer (meniscofemoral and meniscotibial ligaments) is a thickening of the capsule attached to the medial meniscus. The superficial MCL inserts on the tibia 5–7 cm below the joint line, under the pes anserinus — the distal attachment may be outside the field of view on some series (inferred; depends on protocol).

**Baker's — the subject is a bursa, not the capsule.** A Baker's (popliteal) cyst is distension of the gastrocnemius–semimembranosus bursa, which communicates with the joint through a posteromedial capsular slit. It is defined by its neck passing *between the medial head of gastrocnemius and the semimembranosus tendon*, best seen axially. It is posteromedial, not generically posterior. Posterior fluid that lacks that neck is something else: a parameniscal cyst (adjacent to a meniscal tear, at the joint line), a cruciate ganglion, a popliteal artery aneurysm (a vascular lumen, with flow void), a nerve sheath tumor, or another bursa. You already noted muscle/tendon bounding; the correction is that the muscle and tendon are not incidental boundaries, they are the definition.

**"Zero of the twelve is about a bone as such"** — this is not true of your own table: Fracture and Contusion are predicates on a bone's interior and cortex, and OA's osteophytes, subchondral cysts, and sclerosis are bone predicates too. The honest statement is: two of the twelve are bone predicates; the other ten use bones as scaffolding.

## 2. The fibula claim

**Anatomically, true without exception.** The fibula is lateral (and slightly posterior) to the tibia in every human leg; there is no situs inversus of a limb. The only "exceptions" are absence (fibular hemimelia, amputation), which correctly produce abstention.

**Where the resolved territory can mislead** — all pipeline, not anatomy:

- **Coverage.** The fibular head articulates with the posterolateral tibia 1–2 cm below the lateral plateau. Coronal stacks often include it only in posterior slices; sagittal stacks only in the lateral-most slices; axial stacks may stop above it. Expect frequent out-of-field, hence frequent abstention, unless a second witness exists.
- **Merge.** The proximal tibiofibular joint's dark band is thin; on a distance-transform watershed the fibular head dome may merge into the tibial territory, or be below the prominence floor. Require that a candidate fibula territory is enclosed by its own closed cortical ring on every slice it occupies, lies inferior to the tibial plateau, and has a long axis parallel to the tibial shaft.
- **The dangerous confusion.** If the watershed splits the tibial plateau into medial and lateral domes (the intercondylar eminence encourages this), a "small lateral-ish tibial territory" could be mistaken for a fibula — and the *medial* plateau is on the wrong side. The closed-ring and below-plateau tests above exclude this.
- **Direction is posterolateral.** Take the tibia→fibula vector, remove its superior–inferior and anterior–posterior components in the LPS frame, and read the sign of what remains. In LPS, +x is patient-left; if the residual points +x, lateral is left, so it is a left knee.

**A stronger idea than the fibula alone: settle all three axes anatomically, never from the tag.** The DICOM patient-position tag (head-first vs feet-first) is a technologist entry; if wrong, the L/R axis sign flips while the geometry stays self-consistent. But every axis has an anatomical witness: superior is the direction the femoral shaft leaves the condyles (the longer shaft); anterior is where the patella sits; lateral is where the fibula sits. LPS is a right-handed frame, so given S and A, L is determined by the cross product. The fibula is then a *cross-check* rather than the sole witness: if the fibula disagrees with S×A, the data has been mirrored somewhere and the study should be flagged. This also means laterality can be settled with the fibula out of field, provided the frame handedness is trusted — which you may or may not want to assume.

**Secondary witnesses, all lateral-side, for when the fibula is absent:** the fabella (a sesamoid in the lateral gastrocnemius head, posterolateral to the lateral femoral condyle, present in roughly a quarter of people, a closed-cortex ovoid above the joint line); the popliteus tendon (dark cord in a groove on the lateral femoral condyle); the medial femoral condyle being larger with a longer anteroposterior dimension; the medial tibial plateau being concave and the lateral convex. The shape witnesses are statistical tendencies, not invariants; use them only as concurrence, not alone.

## 3. Missing elements

Each is named with the finding that needs it.

- **Articular cartilage layers** (femoral condyles, trochlea, tibial plateaus, patellar facets) — all three OA rows; also meniscus and fracture (osteochondral fragments).
- **Subchondral plate** as a distinct object (the dark lamina under cartilage) — OA (cysts, sclerosis, edema), fracture (plateau depression, insufficiency line), contusion.
- **Osteophytes** — OA, as a bone sub-object at the margin.
- **PCL** — ACL (buckling as a secondary sign; disambiguating which band in the notch is which), Baker's (cruciate ganglion distractor).
- **Meniscal roots, horns, body, meniscocapsular junction** — both meniscus rows.
- **Meniscofemoral ligaments (Humphrey, Wrisberg), transverse intermeniscal ligament, popliteus tendon and its hiatus** — lateral meniscus (every one of these creates a pseudo-tear at the posterior or anterior horn).
- **Deep MCL, MCL bursa, pes anserinus, medial retinaculum/MPFL** — MCL (bursal fluid and pes bursitis are medial fluid that is not MCL injury).
- **LCL, biceps femoris, iliotibial band** — not scored, but they define the lateral wall and the Segond region for ACL.
- **Hoffa's (infrapatellar) fat pad, suprapatellar fat pad, prefemoral fat pad** — effusion (they bound the recesses) and synovitis (Hoffa edema is the standard non-contrast synovitis surrogate).
- **Quadriceps tendon and patellar tendon** — effusion (suprapatellar recess walls), PF OA (patellar tracking).
- **Suprapatellar recess, medial and lateral gutters, posterior femoral recesses, suprapatellar plica** — effusion, synovitis.
- **Medial gastrocnemius head, semimembranosus tendon, popliteal artery and vein, tibial nerve** — Baker's.
- **Named bursae** (prepatellar, pes anserine, semimembranosus–tibial collateral, MCL bursa) — effusion and Baker's as non-communicating fluid distractors.
- **Physeal scar, bipartite patella, bone islands, red marrow** — fracture and contusion distractors.
- **Fabella** — laterality witness; also a bone-like object the residual will produce.
- **Loose bodies** — OA; also fracture.

## 4. The residual, mechanically

**The central tool is a tissue fingerprint by conjunction across co-registered series, not a threshold on any one.** This is your concurrence law applied to signal. Known MRI physics:

- Fat: bright on non-suppressed T1 and T2; dark on fat-suppressed. So fat = bright(non-FS) ∧ dark(FS).
- Fluid: dark on T1; bright on T2; bright on FS T2/PD. So fluid = bright(FS) ∧ bright(T2 non-FS) ∧ dark(T1).
- Cortical bone, tendon, ligament, meniscus (fibrocartilage): dark on everything.
- Hyaline cartilage: intermediate on PD/T1; intermediate-to-bright on PD FS; always lower than fluid, always higher than meniscus. (Caveat: on gradient-echo sequences cartilage is bright; the regime key must know the sequence type from TR/TE/flip angle/ScanOptions/InversionTime, not from series description text.)
- Muscle: intermediate on T1, intermediate-dark on T2 and FS.
- Marrow edema: dark on T1, bright on FS, inside a bone territory.
- Synovium: intermediate on everything non-contrast; bright on T1 FS after gadolinium.

Internal references avoid absolute levels: subcutaneous fat and marrow fat are the fat reference, muscle is the intermediate reference, and a distended recess (when present) is the fluid reference. "Bright" and "dark" can then be stated relative to those witnesses in the same image.

**Cartilage.** Along outward normals from each resolved subchondral surface facing an interstice, sample the profile; cartilage is the non-dark layer of intermediate class between the dark plate and whatever lies beyond (fluid, meniscus, or opposing cartilage). The thickness map on the plate surface is the object. The compartment on the opposite side of the same knee is the natural control: medial vs lateral tibiofemoral cartilage compared within the same study is a concurrence with no external threshold. Full-thickness loss = plate facing the interstice with no intermediate layer.

**Menisci.** On coronal and sagittal slices, the meniscus is a dark-on-all-sequences triangle at the periphery of a tibiofemoral interstice: base at the capsule, apex pointing toward the notch, sitting on the tibial plateau cartilage. Dark-on-all distinguishes it from cartilage and fluid; peripheral position distinguishes it from the cruciates. Axially it is C-shaped. Sub-objects: anterior horn, body, posterior horn, roots (the posterior root inserts just anterior to the PCL tibial insertion, medially; laterally just behind the intercondylar eminence). The tear predicate: a non-dark line inside the triangle that reaches the superior or inferior surface, on two adjacent slices — this "two-slice" criterion is a published rule and natively a concurrence. Shape predicates: truncation (missing apex), displaced dark fragment in the notch or a gutter, extrusion beyond the tibial plateau margin measured in mm. Pseudo-tears are resolved by resolving their causes as objects: the popliteus tendon passing through the posterolateral meniscus, the meniscofemoral ligament attachment, the transverse ligament attachment.

**ACL and PCL.** Inside the notch, exactly two dark bands connect femur to tibia. The one attached to the posteromedial surface of the *lateral* femoral condyle (notch lateral wall) and running anteroinferiorly to the anterior intercondylar area is the ACL; the one from the medial condyle's notch wall curving posteroinferiorly to the posterior tibia is the PCL (thicker, more uniformly dark). Predicates: continuity of the band between its two footprints; angle relative to the notch roof (Blumensaat line); intra-band FS-bright signal; absence (empty notch sign). Secondary geometric signs, all measurable from resolved bones: anterior tibial translation (posterior cortex of the lateral tibial plateau relative to the posterior lateral femoral condyle on sagittal; published 5–7 mm figures), PCL buckling, uncovered posterior horn of the lateral meniscus, pivot-shift contusions, Segond fragment. Reconstructed ACLs (graft plus femoral and tibial tunnels — fluid- or dark-lined channels through bone) must be recognized as a separate object class or the parse will fire "fracture" on the tunnels.

**Collaterals.** The MCL is the dark band hugging the medial contour of the femur–tibia union on coronal, immediately deep to subcutaneous fat, spanning the joint line, superficial to the medial meniscus. Predicates: discontinuity; FS-bright signal within or sleeving it; thickness; waviness. The LCL is the dark cord from lateral epicondyle to fibular head (needs the fibula territory). Distractors as objects: the MCL bursa (fluid between superficial and deep layers), pes anserine bursa.

**Capsule and recesses.** See section 6. Mechanically: the synovial cavity is the fluid-class connected component contiguous with the tibiofemoral interstices; its named recesses are its portions superior to the patella (suprapatellar), medial/lateral to the condyles (gutters), and posterior to the condyles. Hoffa's fat pad is the fat-class region posterior to the patellar tendon, anterior to the notch, inferior to the patella — one of the easiest objects in the knee to resolve. The quadriceps tendon and patellar tendon are dark bands attached to the patella.

**Baker's.** A fluid-class component posteromedial to the knee whose neck lies between the muscle-class medial gastrocnemius head and the dark semimembranosus tendon on axial. Vessels are excluded as objects by flow void (dark lumen on spin-echo) or by their tubular course continuous across many slices.

**Bone sub-objects.** The watershed already tends to split the distal femur into two condyles and a shaft (the notch is a saddle), and the tibia into two plateaus (the eminence is a saddle). That split is not a defect — it is the compartment enumeration. The notch walls, trochlea, epicondyles, tibial spines, tibial tubercle, Gerdy's tubercle, and fibular head are then named by relations (superior/inferior/anterior/posterior/lateral to each other) in the settled frame.

## 5. Where "a predicate on a resolved object" fails

Four places, with four different obstacles.

**Synovitis — the acquisition does not carry the information.** Addressed in 6 and 7.

**Contusion — the predicate is non-unique on its subject.** Marrow edema is one object property with five causes. The parse can give you edema's location and its relations (is there a fracture line through it? a cartilage defect over it? a matching edema in the opposing bone?), and the founder's diagnostic recall can sort the pattern. But "predicate on a resolved object" alone does not settle it; the settling is a predicate on *relations among several objects*. That still lives in the model, but note it is a different shape than "look at the object, measure one thing."

**Menisci and MCL — label semantics, not physics.** I infer (I do not know) that the RSNA labels are derived from radiology reports. A post-meniscectomy meniscus is truncated — the shape predicate fires — but the report says "postoperative change," and the label may be negative. A grade-1 MCL sprain may or may not be labeled positive depending on the dataset's convention. Here going finer cannot help, because the obstacle is a convention about what the word "tear" or "injury" denotes, which is not in the image. The right response under your laws is to decide what the predicate measures, state it, and accept that the gold label's convention is external — never tune to it.

**Any finding at the field-of-view edge — the information is not acquired.** The distal MCL insertion, the fibula, the inferior extent of a Baker's cyst, and the superior extent of the suprapatellar recess can all be cut off. Abstention is correct; the point is that no amount of object refinement recovers an unacquired voxel.

One structural note on the metric, which is "something else": macro ROC-AUC has no abstain state. A silent case must be given *some* rank. If the decomposition abstains on most synovitis cases, synovitis AUC tends toward 0.5 regardless of how good the positive decisions are. This is not a reason to change the laws; it is a reason to know, per finding, how often the parse will be silent, and to be explicit about what rank silence receives.

## 6. Capsule and synovium

**Known:** the fibrous capsule is a thin dark membrane that is not reliably separable from adjacent dark structures (retinacula, tendons, deep MCL) on routine MRI. It is reliably resolvable only where fluid distends it — i.e., when there is an effusion. So "the capsule's boundary mapped on all images" is not achievable deterministically as a tissue object in the normal knee.

**Closest defensible subject:** the synovial cavity defined by what it contains and what it is bounded by. Contents: the fluid-class component connected to the tibiofemoral interstices. Bounds: the bone surfaces, the three fat pads (Hoffa, suprapatellar, prefemoral), the quadriceps tendon, the patella, the posterior condyles, the gastrocnemius origins. Within that, the "separable lining tissue" is the intermediate-class layer between fluid and bound — resolvable only where fluid exists to separate it from the wall, and only when thicker than about one voxel (in-plane roughly 0.3–0.6 mm, through-plane 3–4 mm; inferred typical values). Frond-like projections of intermediate tissue into fluid, and Hoffa's fat pad showing FS-bright signal within fat, are the two deterministic non-contrast synovitis witnesses. They are exactly what the published non-contrast scoring systems use: the MOAKS system combines effusion and synovitis into a single "effusion-synovitis" item precisely because non-contrast MRI cannot separate them, and scores "Hoffa synovitis" separately as the fat pad signal.

**A concrete regime-key idea.** In routine knee protocols, a T1-weighted *fat-suppressed* series is almost never acquired without gadolinium. If the regime key finds a T1 FS series (short TE, short TR, fat-suppression tag set), that series is probably post-contrast, and on it bright synovium against dark fluid is direct enhancement evidence — vessels in the same image serve as the enhancement reference. The trap: MR arthrography also uses T1 FS, with *intra-articular* gadolinium, and the joint is deliberately distended — such a study will read as a massive effusion and the enhancement logic inverts. The tell is that the fluid itself is bright on T1 FS. Flag both cases from the regime key before any predicate runs.

## 7. Does going finer absorb every objection?

For structural findings — fracture, OA, menisci, ACL, MCL, effusion, Baker's — yes. Each refinement in sections 1–4 is another physical object with a resolvable role, and the obstacles are coverage and partial volume, which are honest abstention conditions.

It stops helping at exactly one finding and for one reason. **Synovitis:** the distinguishing property of the finest object, "separable lining tissue," is either enhancement (requires an agent the routine acquisition does not contain) or thickness (requires the lining to span more than one voxel, which normal and mildly inflamed synovium does not). No finer object fixes that; the information is absent from the voxels or below their size. The decomposition remains correct as a description — the founder is right that recess, fluid, and lining are three objects — but the predicate on the third object is undecidable in the typical study, and the model must say "unknown" there rather than "no." The refinement absorbs the *conceptual* objection and leaves the *acquisition* objection standing, which is the proper outcome under "unknown is never false."

A second, weaker stopping point: **contusion's cause** and **the meniscal/MCL label convention** stop at semantics, not at objects. Going finer gives better inputs to diagnostic recall; it does not make the predicate settle itself.

## 8. The finer objects, per finding

Substrate used by all twelve: femur (shaft, medial condyle, lateral condyle, trochlea, notch roof and walls, epicondyles, adductor tubercle), tibia (shaft, medial plateau, lateral plateau, intercondylar eminence with spines, anterior and posterior intercondylar areas, tibial tubercle, Gerdy's tubercle), patella (median ridge, medial and lateral facets), fibula (head, styloid, neck); for each, cortex (dark shell), subchondral plate, marrow (fat vs red marrow), physeal scar; the LPS frame with S, A, L settled anatomically.

**Fracture:** cortex continuity per bone; subchondral plate (depression, step-off); trabecular marrow; fracture line (dark on all sequences); surrounding marrow edema; separated fragments (Segond, tibial spine, patellar, osteochondral); lipohemarthrosis (fat layer floating on fluid layer in the suprapatellar recess — pathognomonic for intra-articular fracture); periosteal and soft-tissue edema; distractors as objects: physeal scar, bipartite patella, bone island, enchondroma, ACL graft tunnels.

**Contusion:** each bone region's marrow; edema region (T1 dark ∧ FS bright); its position against named sub-regions (lateral femoral sulcus terminalis, posterolateral tibial plateau, anterior femur/tibia); the overlying cartilage (intact or not); subchondral plate (intact); fracture line (absent); red marrow as distractor; subchondral OA edema as distractor; osteonecrosis as distractor.

**Medial / lateral tibiofemoral OA:** the two opposing subchondral plates; the two cartilage layers with thickness maps and full-thickness areas; marginal osteophytes on femur and tibia; subchondral cysts (fluid class inside bone); subchondral sclerosis; subchondral edema; the meniscus (extrusion, degeneration); loose bodies; synovial fluid; limb axis (varus/valgus from femoral and tibial shaft axes); tibial slope; intercondylar notch narrowing; the opposite compartment as internal control.

**Patellofemoral OA:** patellar facets and median ridge cartilage; trochlear groove cartilage; trochlear depth (dysplasia); patellar tilt and lateral subluxation relative to the trochlea; patellar osteophytes; subchondral cysts and edema in patella and trochlea; lateral retinaculum; Hoffa's fat pad (impingement edema); quadriceps and patellar tendons; mediopatellar plica.

**Medial meniscus:** anterior horn, body, posterior horn; anterior and posterior roots and their tibial insertions; meniscocapsular junction and the deep MCL (meniscofemoral and meniscotibial); the two articular surfaces of the meniscus; intrasubstance signal lines and their surface contact; displaced fragments (notch, medial gutter, posterior recess); extrusion past the tibial margin; parameniscal cyst; ramp lesion; post-meniscectomy truncation; chondrocalcinosis; the tibial plateau cartilage it sits on; the opposing femoral cartilage.

**Lateral meniscus:** the same body/horn/root/surface set; the popliteus tendon and popliteomeniscal fascicles at the posterior horn; the meniscofemoral ligaments (Humphrey anterior to, Wrisberg posterior to the PCL) at the posterior horn; the transverse intermeniscal ligament at the anterior horn; discoid morphology; uncovering of the posterior horn (ACL secondary sign); parameniscal cyst; displaced fragments in the lateral gutter.

**ACL:** notch roof (Blumensaat), notch lateral wall, ACL femoral footprint, tibial footprint in the anterior intercondylar area; the ACL band and its anteromedial and posterolateral bundles; its synovial sheath; intra-band edema; PCL (reference, buckling angle); meniscofemoral ligaments; anterior horn of the lateral meniscus; tibial spines; posterior cortex of the lateral tibial plateau vs the posterior lateral femoral condyle (translation); sulcus terminalis and posterolateral tibial contusions; Segond fragment; notch fluid; ACL ganglion and mucoid degeneration as non-tear mimics; graft and femoral/tibial tunnels in reconstructed knees; anterolateral ligament and Kaplan fibers as co-injured neighbors.

**MCL:** superficial MCL (femoral origin at the medial epicondyle; tibial insertion 5–7 cm distal, deep to the pes); deep MCL (meniscofemoral and meniscotibial); MCL bursa; medial meniscus periphery; periligamentous edema in medial soft tissues; medial subcutaneous fat; pes anserinus (sartorius, gracilis, semitendinosus) and its bursa; vastus medialis obliquus; posterior oblique ligament; medial retinaculum and MPFL; adductor tubercle; chronic ossification at the femoral origin (Pellegrini-Stieda); lateral compartment contusions (valgus mechanism); field-of-view edge at the distal insertion.

**Effusion:** the synovial cavity as the connected fluid component: suprapatellar recess, medial and lateral gutters, intercondylar region, posterior femoral recesses; its bounds: quadriceps tendon, suprapatellar fat pad, prefemoral fat pad, Hoffa, patella, trochlea, posterior condyles, gastrocnemius origins; the suprapatellar plica (which may compartmentalize); fluid composition (simple; debris; hemarthrosis; lipohemarthrosis; loose bodies); the normal small physiologic volume as reference; distractors: MR arthrogram, non-communicating bursae (prepatellar, pes, semimembranosus–tibial collateral), ganglia, and the popliteal cyst (communicating but a separate object).

**Synovitis:** the lining as the intermediate-class layer along cavity walls, where fluid separates it; villous and nodular projections into fluid; Hoffa's fat pad edema and mass-like change; suprapatellar fat pad edema; plicae (mediopatellar, suprapatellar, infrapatellar); rice bodies; hemosiderin (dark, blooming on gradient echo — pigmented villonodular synovitis); post-contrast enhancement if a T1 FS series exists, with vessels as reference; the capsule where distended.

**Baker's:** medial head of gastrocnemius; semimembranosus tendon; the bursa between them; its neck through the posteromedial capsule; the fluid body; inferior extension into the calf; rupture (fluid tracking along fascial planes); wall thickness; loose bodies within; distractors: popliteal artery and vein (flow void, tubular course), popliteal aneurysm, parameniscal cyst, cruciate ganglion, nerve sheath tumor on the tibial nerve, other medial bursae.

**Uncertainties, stated plainly.** I do not know the RSNA label provenance, the sequence mix per study, whether any post-contrast series exist, or the typical fields of view; everything I said about those is inference from standard practice. The anatomy and MRI signal behavior above is known. The claim that the watershed splits condyles and plateaus is inference from the geometry of the distance transform, not observation of your output.
