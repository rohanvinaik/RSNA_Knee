## The answer, verbatim

1. **The workflow is substantially supported; several proposed rules remain unruled.** These judgments concern the quoted contracts, not demonstrated performance.

   **Step 0 — SUPPORTED.** The settled record supplies the loader's "millimetre geometry" and the five acquisitions. Recording the same-orientation pairs follows that record. The reliability of sequence identification is not demonstrated here.

   **Step 1 — SUPPORTED in purpose; LEFT OPEN in specification.** The founder explicitly endorses the squint for cheap localization. Millimetre tiles are consistent with that purpose, but their size and placement are the lead's inference. "A tile's median is exactly that tile's median" warrants the measurement, not preservation of every anatomical distinction.

   **Step 2 — LEFT OPEN as a rule; partly CONTRADICTED as a contract.** Border connection is an admissible candidate, not an established warrant: the founder says authority comes from "being correct." Neither darkness nor border connection proves that a mixed tile contains no anatomy. Passing only leg tiles also excludes abstentions, contrary to "Its silence settles nothing."

   **Step 3 — SUPPORTED in principle; LEFT OPEN in coverage.** Keeping acquisition digits separate follows "The digits stay separate; a question reads the conjunction." The record does not establish that every orientation's pair supplies every conjunct required by the tissue table. Nearest centres and slices are a proposed correspondence rule, not proof of identical anatomical support.

   **Step 4 — LEFT OPEN.** Peaks and territories are supported, as is selection through structural facts. "Two largest," the patella's positional criterion, and the fibula's proposed criterion are not confirmed selection laws. The record requires facts, including the shaft and closed ring, whose computation and sufficiency are unspecified.

   **Step 5 — SUPPORTED for targeted refinement; LEFT OPEN as an exclusive restriction.** The founder authorizes expensive boundary work "where they actually look like they might exist." That does not establish that all subsequent native work can be confined to boundary strips. The outline operation must preserve "the body's area and topology"; which body supplies those constraints remains unspecified.

   **Step 6 — SUPPORTED as a contextual round; LEFT OPEN as an object rule.** Objects inside a constructed interstice instantiate "the same kind of problem with added context." This is not intrinsically a departure from the thesis. However, darkness alone does not establish the enclosing structure, identity or separation of each new object.

   **Step 7 — LEFT OPEN.** Its subjects serve the settled findings, but the span selectors and connected-envelope rule are additional domain specifications, not consequences established by the quotations.

   **Step 8 — SUPPORTED in purpose; LEFT OPEN operationally.** Residual ownership matches the record. The plan does not specify how competing claims settle or how this late residual relates to pull-out between earlier rounds.

   **Step 9 — SUPPORTED as a contract; LEFT OPEN as executable diagnosis.** Object/relation predicates and named abstentions follow the record. Required readings, selectors and expected readings remain incomplete.

   No step explicitly writes an anatomical kind-label into a record or dispatches on one. "Indexed objects" carrying selection facts is consistent with the rule. The anatomical names can remain design glosses; the eventual schemas and predicate selectors are not shown, so compliance cannot yet be verified.

3. **The following contract errors can be proved without seeing images.**

   - **Abstention exclusion:** Step 2 permits three disjoint outcomes but sends only leg tiles onward. Therefore any abstained tile receives the same computational exclusion as background. The thesis explicitly says silence settles nothing.
   - **The proposed swap creates a cycle:** Step 3 consumes leg tiles from Step 2. Under §7's alternative, Step 2 consumes Step 3's conjunction. Merely exchanging their positions cannot satisfy both dependencies.
   - **A declared input lacks a declared producer:** Step 6 consumes the facing-pair graph; Steps 0–5 do not produce it. Existing "sampled pairings" are mentioned, but their identity with, and supply of, this graph are not specified.
   - **Border connection is not sufficient by itself:** A tile can contain a minority of anatomical pixels while its median falls below the valley. If that tile connects through qualifying tiles to the border, the candidate clears it. Both clauses hold while anatomy is present. This disproves an unconditional guarantee, not the rule's measured performance on these images.

   The eight-millimetre arithmetic is correct under the stated example: sixteen pixels at half a millimetre each. A 256 × 256 image reduced to 16 × 16 has 256 times fewer samples. That does not establish square-root cost "for EVERYTHING," including construction of the squint.

4. **The build order is not the completed-step order forced by the inputs.**

   Step 2 consumes Step 1's squint, so a completed Step 2 cannot pass its specified real-entry-point gate before that input exists. Its decision function could be developed earlier, but that is different from completing the stated step.

   The declared chain is 0 → 1 → 2 → 3 → 4 → 5 → 6 → 7, with Step 8 collecting claims and Step 9 consuming the resulting object readings. Step 6's graph source remains missing. The Step 2/3 alternative has no executable ordering as written.

5. **Only some failures follow from the supplied series descriptions; actual image failures remain unmeasured.**

   The axial series necessarily gets an abstained conjunction under Step 3. Step 4 then lacks its required conjunction classifications, and its two-long-bone selector does not describe the axial anatomy acknowledged in §7.

   On any series, anatomy occupying less than the decisive share of a tile can disappear from its median. This can invalidate background clearance or erase a small content object; the meniscus concern is therefore substantive.

   On the coronal and sagittal pairs, a required tissue conjunction cannot resolve when its acquisition evidence is absent; "fat-bright" and "fat-dark" alone do not establish every sequence role.

   Across slices, the largest territories need not retain the same anatomical identity. The plan supplies no demonstrated rule for that case.

   Native interior findings cannot consume native interior digits produced only in edge strips. Whether Step 9 instead intends coarse counts remains open. Post-contrast availability likewise cannot be determined from the five names.

## The lead's comparison, written after reading the answer

**Agreement, reached independently.** The funnel's shape (squint for targeting, native along edges,
the first lens frozen, the free lenses by place, objects grounded at peaks, interstices constructed,
contents located by them, the residual last) is supported by the founder's words and the thesis;
no step writes anatomy into a record or dispatches on a kind-label. Codex's §5 failures on the axial
series and on small bodies vanishing in a tile's median are the lead's §7 uncertainties, reached
independently.

**Provable errors Codex reported, which the lead accepts as its own corrections.**

1. *Abstention exclusion.* The first lens has three outcomes, and the lead's plan passed only the leg
   tiles on. That treats an abstention as a clearing, against "Its silence settles nothing"
   (§1.11). Corrected: later steps receive every tile the first lens did not positively clear, leg
   and abstained alike; only a tile the lens declared background is excluded. That is the senior
   filter's standing exactly: it decides alone where it fires, and settles nothing where it is silent.
2. *The swap is a cycle.* The lead's §7 said that on the fat-dark views the first lens might need the
   conjunction, so steps 2 and 3 would swap; but step 3's digits are read over the leg tiles step 2
   produces. Corrected by splitting step 3: 3a lines the two squints up by place on the knee (geometry
   only, no population needed) and 3b reads the digits over the leg. The order becomes 1, 3a, 2, 3b.
   Where an orientation has one acquisition, 3a is silence and step 2 reads one squint.
3. *The facing-pair graph has no producer.* Added as step 5b: two objects face when gap tiles (dark on
   every acquisition, or background) touch both and no third object lies between; the gap tiles that
   touch both are the pair's record. The existing sampled pairings are a different record and are not
   this graph.
4. *Border connection is not sufficient.* A tile at the leg's rim can hold a minority of knee while its
   median lies below the valley. The lead's candidate rule, stated as the lead's and for his ruling:
   the first lens clears a tile only when every place in it lies at or below the valley and it
   connects to the picture's border through such tiles; a tile with any place above the valley
   abstains. That is a narrower lens, which is the thesis's form for a lens that decides alone. His
   check by eye still governs; this is the candidate he checks.

**The build order, corrected.** Codex is right that step 2 cannot be completed through the real entry
point before the squint it reads exists. The order the inputs force: 0, 1, 3a, 2, 3b, 4, 5, 5b, 6, 7,
8, 9. The pure decision of step 2 can be written and tested first; its sheet, which is what he checks,
needs step 1.

**On the arithmetic.** Codex reads his "(n)^(1/2)" as not covering the cost of making the squint. The
lead's reading: with n the number of places, 256 × 256 = 65,536 places become 256 tiles, and 256 is
the square root of 65,536, so his count is exact for everything computed on the squint; the squint
itself is one pass over the places, made once and kept. Both readings agree on the number.

**Two gaps Codex found that the lead's plan did not state, taken as additions to the plan.**

- *The third dimension.* The plan was written at the selected slice. The largest territory at one
  slice need not be the same object at the next. The squint is cheap enough to run over every slice
  of a series, so an object is its territories through the slices it occupies, linked slice to
  slice by overlap; that is the thesis's column and band (§6.4), re-derived for a bone. Stated as
  the lead's, for his ruling.
- *Native readings inside an object.* Step 5 confined native digits to the edge strip, but Contusion
  reads a bone's interior and a small marrow change can vanish in a tile's median. Corrected: native
  digits are made along an object's edge for its boundary, and inside an object's extent when a
  predicate on its interior asks for them; both scoped by the object, never the whole field. Whether
  the squint's conjunction is enough for the interior is a question for him (Codex's third).
