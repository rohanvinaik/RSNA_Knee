The lead’s reading contains a provable overstatement: **the conclave requirement and the engineering requirement are compatible, but their equivalence does not follow.** It also turns freedom from an absent member’s veto into a stronger requirement of computational independence that the founder did not state.

These conclusions concern the supplied document; they do not verify the implementation.

1. **Claim 3 is too strong as written.** A member can legitimately depend on a shared measurement. If that measurement is indispensable to what the member measures, its absence can legitimately make that reading unavailable. That does not imply the other members must stop speaking or that the assembly must collapse.

   The founder objects to one member’s absence defeating the population’s ability to resolve something. He does not forbid dependencies inside a measurement. Indeed, his objection to computing something and then “re-comput[ing it] from scratch” supports sharing measurements rather than requiring each member to obtain everything independently.

   Fact 5 establishes that the digit lens’s current source has a dependency chain and becomes unavailable when coil identity refuses. It also establishes that another measurement remains available. It **does not establish that the alternative measures the same population under every relevant condition**: agreement on two acquisitions is evidence of agreement there, not proof of interchangeability.

2. **Claim 4’s asserted equivalence is unsound.** Its causal argument contains two implications that do not hold:

   - Sequential evaluation does not entail recomputation or loss of previously computed values.
   - Independent readings and population assembly do not entail efficient computation, sharing, or adequate telemetry.

   A computation can retain and share a named value while evaluating dependent operations sequentially. Conversely, independent members can duplicate expensive work. Those possibilities suffice to disprove the claimed equivalence without proposing an architecture.

   The founder explicitly requires telemetry, performance engineering, efficiency, and modular production design **alongside** image output. Conclave semantics address whose absence can prevent resolution and how evidence contributes. They do not, by themselves, satisfy all those engineering obligations. The document does not supply the complete SICP rules, so I cannot adjudicate conformance to those rules specifically.

   Counts alone, however, cannot establish the founder’s “sufficient coverage.” Two populations can have identical counts while their readings cover different places or disagree about the proposed identity. The founder explicitly calls disagreement informative; counts do not preserve its content.

It therefore cannot establish quorum or determine whether unrelated available readings are meaningful. The founder’s first-order telemetry requirement leaves room for execution measurements; his quorum requirement prevents conflating them with evidential completeness.
