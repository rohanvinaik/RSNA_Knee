The founder, 2026-10-14, sent mid-turn while the lead wrote the exact rules of the two processes (recorded
verbatim in `docs/chapters/02-collapse/teachings.md`, "EVERY STEP EXPLAINABLE TO A CHILD"; the project law is
AGENTS.md law 10):
- «Always make sure you're passing a common-sense visual process. Look at the photos. The reasoning you use when
  applying any solution using the lenses should be explainable to a child. We need to identify the elements of
  knee anatomy with common-sense visual reasoning, and diagnosis of the 12 conditions is common-sense reasoning
  based on knee anatomy we can see»
- «This is absurdly simple. If you can't explain it to a child, you don't understand it»
- «Applies to every step of this project»
- Earlier the same day: «make CERTAIN that you're reading my prompts AND actually look at the photos. Those
  photos, plus the <object> thesis, make this a common-sense operation»

**Why:** the lead kept drafting rules as statistics and build vocabulary (domes split by tissue rules, readings
chosen per finding as if they were features) instead of what an eye does with these fixed, almost identical
pictures. The thesis's own claim is that structure makes the hard part simple; a rule that needs a paragraph of
build terms has lost that.

**How to apply:**
- Before writing or building any step, say it in one or two sentences a child would follow ("the line between the
  two bones is the darkest spot with bright above and below"). Then make that sentence exact. If the sentence
  cannot be written, stop and ask him.
- Identify anatomy by look and by fixed place: the two big bones and the dark line between them first, everything
  else by where it always sits next to them.
- Read each finding as the question an eye asks of its element: is the bone cracked or bruised, is the gap thin,
  is the wedge or band lit up, is there fluid where there should not be. How much counts is his.
- Look at the actual pictures (every slice when placing things in depth) before deciding a rule, and look at
  every step's output picture before it goes to him.
