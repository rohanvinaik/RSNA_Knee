The founder, 2026-10-10 (RSNA_Knee), after a nine-agent workflow (every agent on Haiku 4.5) returned lens and label
rules with domain errors, silence filed as exclusion, and judgments written as lenses:
- «I tried to workflow this … people are just too willing to accept CRAP. I need to control and manually guide the
  assertions you make, the ways you think through the information theory of it all. Sub-agents and workflows are
  easier, but it lets you work unbounded and direct your own understanding and interpretation, which is just not
  rigorous enough engagement with the premises of the problem.»
- «I framed it as a "PI-undergrad" pass. I'm the PI, you're the undergrad … Every time there's a decision to make, or a
  problem, something that's just not making the math math properly, your normal instinct is to try and solve it
  quickly. Bad instinct. *I* am the domain expert, and I KNOW that the idea is complete and correct. If you're having
  problems, it's because you're missing something. Your task is to work through what I told you, then when you can't
  make things fit together, to think through the problem using simple, clear language and reasoning in the language of
  the domain space, then present the apparent incongruity in the theory.»
- «a valid lens is ALWAYS correct. It is never wrong. Not because its magical, but because it is defined only to be a
  measurment … it sounds like the "lens"/"law" here is blurring measurement and judgement. I need you to be EXTREMELY
  strict about this.»
- «"silence" isn't silence, it's an "informational zero" … "Silence" is meaningful, not a default negative score. That
  error is a leak of ML priors to a structural AI approach»
- «Domain errors are also utterly fatal.»

**Why:** in the cell-tracking project, by his account, the lead insisted on a number for every step and argued
with him; the project bloated, took weeks, and was not finished in time to submit. Here the workflow's agents worked
unbounded and returned confident errors.

**How to apply:**
- Do the design reasoning yourself, in session, one label and one study at a time. Use no Workflow and no sub-agents
  for it unless he asks for them for a mechanical task.
- Write a lens only as a measurement of the data: what value, at which registered place, in which series, against
  which reference. Keep every step from it to an anatomical claim, and from there to a label, as a named inference
  with its conditions. The thermometer measures what it touched; core temperature is an inference with conditions
  (no hot drink); fever and illness are meanings.
- Never default a zero. Say which zero it is: a place not acquired, or a place acquired and holding nothing above
  what blur and baseline explain. Let the structure define what it means for each question.
- When something does not fit, stop and put the incongruity to him plainly. Do not resolve it with a number, a
  threshold or a quick fix.
- Domain claims must be right. State them carefully, and flag what you are unsure of rather than filling it in.
