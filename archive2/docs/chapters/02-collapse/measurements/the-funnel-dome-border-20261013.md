# The dome's border at the squint: two readings, measured on the one study (2026-10-13)

**The framing below is withdrawn the same day** (the founder: "The "squint" is for localization of
the expensive compute process, not for drawing a line strongly. The other operation is for
fine-drawing of borders. 8mm tiles can't refine the border because that was never their job";
`teachings.md`, "THE TWO PROCESSES"). The counts stand as measurements; the reading of them as a
border to choose at the squint does not, and the two-border seating was stopped mid-repair.

**What was measured.** The lead's probe `lead_dome_probe.py` (the session scratchpad), read-only,
over the saved run `data/measurements/the-funnel-phase-a-20261013` (8 mm tiles, the conjunction
saved on the two fat-dark recipients). For every slice of the two paired recipients, the one shared
measurement `knee.objects.dome.dome` at 8 mm spacing and an 8 mm prominence bar, over two
interiors:

- **Reading A, the rule as built** (`design.md` R5, from the object model's row "cortex, tendon,
  ligament, meniscus: dark on everything"): an obstacle is a tile the first lens cleared, or a
  tile dark on both acquisitions.
- **Reading B, for comparison**: an obstacle is a tile the first lens cleared, or a kept tile whose
  fat-bright partner digit was read and is not `above` (the six read pairs `at_*` and `below_*`).
  Every silence (`partner_silent`, `self_silent`, `unmatched`) and the refusal stay interior,
  because silence settles nothing. (A first version made silent tiles obstacles; the advisor
  caught it, and the counts below are from the corrected rule. They moved by a few tiles at most.)

**Why it was measured.** Two builders and three checkers reported independently that on the
coronal recipient's slice 15 the built rule gives one territory. The U3c builder saw why on the
sheets: at 8 mm the two big bodies read `above_at` (bright on the fat-bright, in the middle third
on the fat-dark, whose terciles are read over every tile the lens kept, the dark skin-line ring
included), and the line between them reads `at_below`, with `below_below` only at its medial end.

**The counts** (territories with their sizes in tiles, largest first; "one" means a single
territory holds the interior, small seeded specks of one or two tiles aside):

| series | slices | reading A | reading B |
|---|---|---|---|
| coronal fat-dark, middle slices 11 to 19 | 9 | one territory on 8 (two only on slice 14: 157 and 123) | two large territories on all 9 (second 47 to 59 tiles) |
| sagittal fat-dark, middle slices 10 to 21 | 12 | one territory on 10 (two on slices 16 and 17: 223/225 and 88) | two territories of 30 tiles or more on all 12 |

At the slice the sheets show today: coronal slice 15, A one territory of 280 tiles, B 106 and 54
with smaller ones of 6 and 3; sagittal slice 18, A one of 316, B 67 and 63 with an 18-tile third
territory and smaller ones.

**What it means, labelled as the lead's inference.** At 8 mm the dark line between the two bones
is narrower than a tile, so no tile row reads dark on both, and a border read as "dark on both"
does not part the bones. The bright bodies themselves are many tiles across, and the tiles
between them are not bright on the fat-bright, so a border read as the edge of the bright body
parts them on every middle slice of both views. Nothing here settles which reading is the
founder's; the object model's table is his record and reading A follows it literally.

**What is being done with it** (to be amended once done). Reading A is his recorded table, so it
is not replaced: under his ruling of 2026-10-12 ("we always keep information we compute ...
keep everything, including where things conflict"), both readings are seated as two members. The
border becomes an argument of the dome, the route reads both domes on every chosen slice, every
stage below the dome is read once per dome, and every saved entry names its border, so his sheets
show both side by side and he chooses at E3. The axial has no fat-bright partner, so both members
read its own rule there. The counts go to him in plain words.
