## The thesis's two processes, in its own words

The squint and the cheap readings point: "These readings point, and never decide alone ... It
narrows where the expensive work runs" (§5.1); the squint's bodies "outline the cells poorly, but
they lie on the cells, and over the open field no lens draws at all" (§4.6); its questions are
categorical, "whether there is a shape here or only blur" (§4.5). The exact border is drawn at full
resolution: the outline pulled taut, "No border is known more finely than the flicker" (§6.1), and
the last look reads each object "on the untouched light" (§5.7). His words are the same two
things: "(1) high-level localization and targeting, and (2) fine-grained targeting and
boundary-drawing".

## What the pictures show, structure by structure

The layout through each stack is fixed, as he said ("knee images? They almost ALWAYS look the
same"):

- **Coronal, front to back:** the kneecap seen face-on as an oval; the front of the upper bone with
  the pouch above the kneecap (bright on the fat-dark); the joint, with the two bones, the band
  between them, a dark wedge at each end of the band and a thin dark line along the inner side;
  the notch in the middle of the band with dark bands crossing it, and the small bone below the
  outer edge of the lower bone; the back of the two rounded ends of the upper bone; the soft tissue
  behind the knee.
- **Sagittal, side to side:** soft tissue; one compartment (a rounded end over the flat top of the
  lower bone, with dark wedges at the front and back of the band); the middle, with the kneecap in
  front, the notch and the dark bands crossing it, and the pouch above the kneecap; the other
  compartment; soft tissue.
- **Axial, bottom to top:** the lower bone with the small bone beside it; the joint level; the
  kneecap over the groove of the upper bone, with bright fluid between; the pouch above.

So every structure the twelve findings need has a fixed place relative to the two giant objects
and the band between them, and a fixed range of slices.

## Where the build departs from that

1. **The slice to read.** The four cheap readings ask nothing about where the knee is, and pick
   the ends of the stacks, which are tangential cuts. In the pictures the slices to read are where
   the structures are: the joint slices for the two bones and their band, each compartment's slices
   for its wedges, the middle slices for the kneecap, the notch and the pouch.
2. **The other objects.** The kneecap is the bright body in front of the lower end of the upper
   bone, above the band; the small outer bone is the bright body below the outer edge of the lower
   bone, toward the back. The build finds them by rules on a dome's territories (the two largest,
   a four-tile floor, position tests) instead of by where they sit relative to the two halves.
3. **The spaces between.** The band between the two halves is the space between the two bones,
   and the band between the kneecap and the front of the upper bone is the other one. The build
   rebuilds "between" from tile projections instead of reading the band the squint already finds.
4. **What lies in the spaces, the inner-side line and the fluid.** The wedges at the band's ends,
   the bands crossing the notch and the thin line along the inner side are a few millimetres
   across, so they are drawn by the native lenses inside the places the squint found. The build
   reads them as dark 8 mm tiles. The fluid in the pouches is large and bright on the fat-dark
   pictures, so the squint can point at it; its edge and its lining are native work.
5. **The side.** The outer side is where the small outer bone is, on the back coronal slices and the
   low axial slices.

## The shape that follows

The squint, on every slice and cheaply, says where each structure is: the first lens, the two
halves and the band between them by the header's up-down axis, and the rest by their fixed places
relative to those, with buffer, and in depth by the slices where they sit. The native lenses, only
there, draw each structure's border, the band's walls, what lies in the band, the inner-side line
and the fluid's edge. The twelve are read off what the native lenses drew.
