## The answer, verbatim

1. The distance dome follows the idea of finding interior peaks and retaining their territories. Making an **undecided skin tile an obstacle**, however, assigns it a structural role the first lens did not establish. Keeping its data does not keep it eligible for an object's interior. That conflicts with the approved buffer staying in play and could interrupt the small anterior object near the skin. Treating `below_below` as a boundary is plausible for bright bone interiors, but those same words also describe dark objects in the settled record; this dome cannot resolve all objects indiscriminately. The axial median split and 8 mm prominence requirement are additional decisions. "Nothing cut on strength" does not establish that every peak shorter than one tile may be denied a territory.

2. Structural selection and scanner coordinates follow the record; +x meaning patient left is correct. The particular selectors do not plainly follow it. "Two giant objects" does not establish "the two largest territories on every chosen slice," nor a four-tile floor. Four complete tiles represent 256 mm²: a substantial exclusion for small or partially intersected objects. A patella is anterior to the femur, but its centre is not necessarily strictly between the tibial top and the femoral centre. That interval is not a dependable anatomical definition of the patella. The coronal selector likewise does not establish that a smaller, ringed, inferior, offset territory is the fibular head. The laterality sign follows **if that witness is correctly selected**; the selection itself remains unestablished. The axial ranking is another unapproved assumption.

3. Restricting expensive boundary work to candidate objects plainly follows the founder's words. Restricting it to **only tile-edge midpoints**, with a fixed ±6 mm reach, is a further decision. A territory boundary has not yet been shown to locate the physical boundary within that reach. Sampling discrete midpoints can also miss a focal break between them; that matters for fracture. Object-specific terciles follow his approval. "Nothing else" receiving a native reading needs clarification against contract 5's thin contents and contract 6's lining: these contracts do not establish that those structures can be resolved without one. The deferred outline might address some limitations, but its absent contract cannot serve as evidence that they are addressed.

4. Recording facing relationships, contact and a saddle follows the relational thesis. Requiring one obstacle tile to touch both objects does not follow "the interstice exists the moment its two bounding objects do." Two bones can plainly face across a gap wider than one tile. Conversely, an obstacle touching both need not establish the anatomical space between them, particularly when obstacles include undecided skin tiles. Contract 5 therefore receives only a restricted subset of the interstices the settled record says already exist. Naming direct contact is useful, but if contact prevents construction of the interstice, a narrowed or closed joint space can lose the object on which its violation must be read.

5. Constructing an interstice from resolved objects follows the record. Its particular straight centre-axis extent, width overlap, wall sequences and inner-half division are decisions the quoted words do not settle. Knee joint surfaces are curved; a straight overlap region is not automatically their space. The clearest anatomical problem is treating few-millimetre menisci or ligament structures as resolved bodies of **8 mm median-classified tiles**. Their signal can disappear into the tile's surrounding material, while a dark tile can include several different structures. The contract can report those tile bodies; it has not established them as the intended contents. The modal fill is a measurement, not evidence that the whole extent has that fill. The nine-word restriction also needs reconciliation with the conjunction's thirteen outputs, whose meanings are not supplied here.

6. Using the fat-dark acquisition alone for the fluid reading **plainly follows the settled record**: fluid is its own `above` reading. Requiring a paired fluid signature would contradict that record. The unsettled part is turning one connected bright component touching an interstice into the envelope. Visible fluid does not by itself map the capsule's complete boundary or its lining; separate collections and thin connections can also be lost at this scale. "Posterior of both objects" does not uniquely identify the posterior pouch, and the narrowest tile row is not established as its neck. The span measurement follows the outside-the-space relation, but contains no medial qualification. It therefore does not yet establish the MCL gloss. Its exclusion also inherits any erroneous interstice extent from contract 5.

7. Cheap readings choosing slices follows the founder's confirmed words, including "not every slice." The particular maximum rules are not thereby confirmed. The eight-readable-slice floor and middle-of-ties choice contradict the supplied cell trace's no-floor and earliest-tie behaviour; retaining every object tie is also different from collapsing ties to one slice. A middle chosen from separated tied slices could even be a slice where the reading did not peak, depending on what "middle" means. Eight slices is a coverage requirement, not anatomical evidence. These differences need resolution before the already-built slice choices can be treated as the same contract. I cannot determine the full compatibility with the depth stage, or the first lens's three and conjunction's thirteen words, because their complete output contracts are absent.

My highest-confidence objections are the undecided buffer's exclusion from interiors, the one-gap-tile condition on interstice existence, and the conflicting slice-choice rules. The anatomical selectors and thin-content resolution are unestablished claims, rather than demonstrated failures on supplied images.

Four questions for the founder:

- Have the four-tile floor and the sagittal position tests earned your approval through what you have seen, or are they still the lead's guesses?
- Do these tile bodies count as resolved thin contents, and does the bright component count as the capsule boundary you meant?

## The lead's comparison, written after reading the answer, and what changed

**Two corrections taken into the contracts the same hour, as provable against his words.**

1. *The buffer at the skin.* The lead's dome made an undecided tile an obstacle; Codex: that
   "conflicts with the approved buffer staying in play and could interrupt the small anterior
   object near the skin." The lead had flagged the same doubt in its own reading. His words
   ("some buffer is perfectly fine"; "they stay in play") settle it: a kept tile is eligible for an
   object's interior. `dome_tile_code` now makes only a cleared tile an obstacle outright; an
   undecided tile follows the kept tiles' rule. The record gains `cleared` so a later stage can
   tell a dark obstacle from the air.
2. *A gap wider than one tile.* The lead's facing rule needed one obstacle tile touching both
   objects; Codex: "Two bones can plainly face across a gap wider than one tile", and the cleared
   air must never count. The settled record ("it exists the moment its two bounding objects do")
   settles it. The gap is now the four-connected chain of dark obstacle tiles between the two
   objects' faces, within the overlap of their widths, touching both; the cleared air and the
   buffer are never gap tiles, and the interstice's extent never holds a cleared tile.

**Where the lead agrees and leaves it to him** (named as the lead's in each contract): the
four-tile floor and the sagittal, coronal and axial position tests of the selection; the strip's
6 mm reach and its reading at the tile-boundary midpoints only; the interstice's straight axis
and width overlap, its wall sequences and its inner-half division; the envelope as one connected
bright body touching an interstice, its pouch and neck; the slice choice's ties and floor (the
depth trace's questions). Each is a provisional rule the pictures will test and he changes by
eye, which is the method he gave ("looking at the output and coming up with arbitrary
lenses/analyses that make the <object> recognition work is valid").

**Where the lead does not follow Codex.** Thin contents at 8 mm: Codex is right that a meniscus
is a few millimetres thick and that an 8 mm tile dark on both acquisitions may hold several
things. The lead's reading: at the squint the contract claims only "a dark body inside the space,
central or peripheral", which is what the tile can say; what the body is at native resolution is a
later round on the strip, as the plan has it. The span's "no medial qualification": the span
carries a side code from the sign, which is the medial qualification; the MCL gloss stays in the
design. The nine read pairs against the conjunction's thirteen words: the fill counts every word
and names the fill from the nine that carry a reading; the four silences cannot fill anything.

**For him, in plain words** (Codex's four, which match the lead's): whether the buffer belongs to
an object (now: yes, by his words); whether the four-tile floor and the position tests have earned
his approval by looking, or stay provisional; whether the dark tile bodies inside the space are
what he means by its contents at this scale; which slice-choice behaviour is his.
