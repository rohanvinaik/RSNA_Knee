# The thesis, in its own words (line numbers in `what_an_object_is.md`)

The project instantiates *What an <Object> Is*, first built for tracking cells in microscopy and stated by the founder to be general. Its elements: LENSES, each a single lossy measurement never wrong about what it measures, whose readings are balanced ternary (below / at / above; assign / abstain / reject) and whose conjunction resolves meaning; the BLACK, the part of the field that never carries what is sought, resolved positively so the rest resolves more cleanly; the SQUINT, a deliberately lossy coarse view; the DOME, an object's interior rising from its surroundings, whose peak grounds it; the PULL-OUT, by which what is resolved leaves the field and the residual is easier; principled abstention; "there is no noise".

- §1.3, 299–313: "We call this move **the squint**: a view's fidelity reduced on purpose, so that the dimension a question reads stands clear of the variation it does not read. That less can answer better is literally true, and it is mathematics … Projecting the reading onto those k dimensions keeps all of the signal and only k of the N shares of variation … so the ratio of signal to variation rises by a factor of N/k … If n places share one level s … the signal-to-noise ratio of the amplitude rises by √n."
- 315–335: "A squinted view is lossless for what it measures. A tile's median is exactly that tile's median, with no error in it … A categorical question accepts any distortion that leaves its answer unchanged."
- §1.5, 488–531: "H₀(X) − H(X | S) = [H₀(X) − H(X)] + I(X; S). The first term is what the laws remove by narrowing the admissible states, and the second is what the rest of the problem says through them … What something is not tells what another thing is … A rejection, 'not X', is warranted by the structure of the problem."
- §1.9, 1050–1074: "remove the plain part of the problem, classified structurally, together with everything its laws say it caused. What remains is the same kind of problem with added context: fewer and more ambiguous <objects>, with the strong signal that masked them gone."
- §1.11, 1177–1189, 1214–1218: "A narrow lens speaks rarely, and is knowably correct where it speaks, so there it settles. Its silence settles nothing … The senior, weak but knowably correct where it speaks, has full authority there. The junior, the stronger reading, refines only where the senior abstains, and never overrules it."
- §4.5, 2247–2257: "a reduced reading is cheaper and cleaner at once, and signal-to-noise rises as the work falls … Reduced to the median light of each 16 × 16 square, a crowded plate still shows where its cells mass and where its ground is dark."
- §4.6, 2280–2295: "Stage 1 clears what any eye would call background before anything is drawn … Each later step works only on what the earlier steps left. Every loss is taken in a view or in how a reading is written, never in what is kept … Stage 1 deletes nothing … reading by exclusion: a reading that is poor at finding a signal can be reliable at saying where the signal is not."
- §5.1, 2325–2336: "cheap readings … Each is made once, in milliseconds, where its input first exists, and is kept for any stage to consult. These readings point, and never decide alone. A coarse view of the whole field is itself a squint … It narrows where the expensive work runs … over a 16 × 16 grid of squares laid on it."
- §5.2, 2340–2357: "by them black is local, never one number … where it fires it is right, and there it decides alone … Stage 1 deletes nothing. Its removals shape the bodies that one lens draws on one slice, and the voxels stay for every other lens and every later round."
- §5.4, 2373–2383: "truth is assigned by **concurrence** … The readings are separate in that each lens measures one thing and knows no question … Two lenses at about one depth read one plane two ways, and each corrects the other's borders … The result is a cleaner set of each lens's own bodies, never a merged picture."
- §5.6, 2434–2485: "What a round leaves is the **residual** … the same kind of problem with added context … The site search follows the composite, which points and never pulls … only the lenses that can say something speak … Reaching down reads the column of each cell just taken, on what is left."
- §6, 2644–2655: "An <object> is a region the structure can enclose, and each border it draws is a law written as geometry … Read as a relief of light, a nucleus is a peak. Its border is where the peak falls to its surroundings, and a valley is where one <object> ends and the next begins."
- §6.1, 2664–2669: "A body's **outline** … is the body's border pulled taut: the trace made as short as it can be while the body's area and topology are kept."
- §6.2, 2699–2711: "A center's **dome** is the territory of places it holds … Nothing is cut on strength, so a bright nucleus never silences a dim one … The dome link … is a cheap prior, drawn by distance alone, and it never decides alone: where it errs is information."
- §7.3, 2980–2990: "Black is silence in the full analysis. Where a lens's stage 1/2 clears a place, that lens spends no full analysis there … Yet a black place still writes something … its cheap readings."
- §13.6, 4296–4308: "Compute once, keep, consult. A value is made once where it becomes known, and handed to every consumer … Physics and abstention prune … A lens that cannot speak at a depth is not computed there."
- §13.7, 4315–4326: "Functions are of three kinds, kept apart. A measurement returns a number or a field, with its scope and provenance. A decision returns an answer or an abstention … An orchestration only moves values between stages … Each use is kept beside it with its consumer, so a finding never used shows itself."

- Anatomy lives in the design, never in the record: objects are indexed, and what selects an object for a rule or a predicate is a structural fact an earlier stage computed (superior/inferior from the header's axis and the shaft, anterior by where the patella sits, lateral by the fibula, what lies across an edge, whether a ring closes), never a kind-label. The names below are design glosses.
- The lens axis is the acquisitions. A tissue class is a conjunction of digits across acquisitions, never a threshold on one image: fat = bright(non-fat-suppressed) ∧ dark(fat-suppressed); fluid = bright(fat-suppressed) ∧ bright(T2) ∧ dark(T1); cortex, tendon, ligament, meniscus = dark on everything; hyaline cartilage = intermediate, below fluid and above meniscus; muscle = intermediate; marrow edema = dark(T1) ∧ bright(fat-suppressed) inside a bone. The digits stay separate; a question reads the conjunction. The middle of each digit is a pair of edges at the terciles of a structurally selected population, read from the image, never an absolute level.

# The lead's plan, written before this consult

## 3. The plan: the funnel, round by round

The route keeps its nine names (study, collapse, lenses, objects, relations, ownership, vectors, predicates, output). What changes is scale, scope and order: every stage after the first lens computes only inside what the stages before it left, the squint does the targeting, native resolution is spent only along edges, and the objects are found in rounds, each round located by the objects the round before resolved. Each step below is one compute operation, or a few, each a pure function of kept readings, parallel over series, tiles or objects, computed once and consulted.

### Step 0. The study, as now
Consumes the competition's files. Produces each series at stored fidelity with its geometry in millimetres from the header: pixel size, slice position, orientation, and the sequence type read from TR/TE/flip angle/inversion time. Adds one fact the funnel needs: which series are the two acquisitions of one orientation (fat-bright and fat-dark), paired. Exists today.

### Step 1. The squint, per series, in millimetres
Consumes a series. Produces the tile grid, each tile the median of its places, with the tile's centre in the scanner's millimetres. The tile size is declared in millimetres, not pixels, so that a tile on one acquisition and a tile on the other cover the same patch of knee whatever their pixel counts. His figure is 16 × 16 on 256 × 256; at half a millimetre a pixel that is a tile about eight millimetres across. The size is his design constant, declared once with the pattern it encodes (coarse enough to absorb a millimetre or two of shift between acquisitions, fine enough to localise a bone), and it is the lead's inference that eight millimetres is what his figure means. Exists today as a pixel-sized grid; the change is the unit. Picture: the squint over the acquired plane.

### Step 2. The first lens: background versus leg, per view, at the squint
Consumes one series' squint and that series' own histogram. Produces, per tile, one decision: background, leg, or an abstention code, with its reason. The candidate rule: a tile is background when its median lies at or below the series' own first valley AND it is connected to the picture's border through such tiles. The second clause is the lead's addition, for the fat-dark views, where marrow is dark and a darkness rule alone could clear the inside of a bone; connection to the border separates air, which touches the frame, from a dark interior, which does not. His check decides: on each of the five views, the lens is right when it marks nothing that is bone or any other part of the knee. Where a view fails, that view gets its own rule, and the rule's provenance is recorded. Once right, the reading is frozen: computed once, saved, handed to every later step, which receives the leg tiles and never computes outside them. The background tiles keep their medians; the decision is a record, not a discard. Exists today: the valley rule and the border-connection idea, both at native resolution; the change is scale and the freeze. Picture: per view, the squint with the background tiles marked, over the acquired plane, so he can check by eye.

### Step 3. The free lenses, lined up, at the squint
Consumes the two squints of one orientation and their geometry. Produces, for every leg tile, the tile of the other acquisition that covers the same millimetres of knee (nearest centre, nearest slice), and for each leg tile one balanced-ternary digit per acquisition against that acquisition's own tercile edges over its own leg tiles. The digit rule exists today at native resolution over the whole volume; the change is scale and population: tiles, inside the leg. The two digits are kept separately. The conjunction of the two is read by the settled table, as a named code per tile; the names of the classes are his. Where an orientation has one acquisition, as the axial does on this study, the second digit is silence and the conjunction abstains with a code. Picture: the two digit grids on the shared frame, side by side, and the conjunction code as colour.

### Step 4. The dome at the squint: the big objects
Consumes the leg tiles, their conjunction codes, and the picture's axes from the header (which way is superior, which is anterior). Produces the interior peaks and the territories at tile scale, and the first indexed objects. The dome's height is distance from what is not bone: from background and from the tiles that are dark on every acquisition (cortex, the gap, tendon). Peaks by prominence, never by a level; each territory is the tiles that climb to one peak. Then the selection by structural facts, which is the lead's reading of "no recognition, pure math": on a coronal or sagittal series the two largest territories are the two big bones, the superior one and the inferior one by the header's axis; on a sagittal series the small territory anterior to the superior bone's end is the patella; on a coronal series a small territory inferior to the inferior bone's upper surface, with its own closed dark ring, is the fibula and the laterality witness. Each object carries its peak, its tiles and the facts that selected it. Exists today (the distance transform, prominence peaks and watershed, at native resolution over the whole plane); the change is scale and the mask. Picture: peaks and territories on the squint, and the selection facts written beside each index.

### Step 5. The edge, at native resolution, only along the edge
Consumes each object's tiles and the native series. Produces the object's boundary, drawn only in a strip a few millimetres wide along the tile boundary where the territory meets a neighbour, the gap or the background. In that strip and nowhere else: the native digit per acquisition (the existing rule, scoped), the native dome and its edges (the existing edges and profile traces, scoped), and the two acquisitions read against each other at the same millimetres (the existing conversation, scoped). The outline is pulled taut as §6.1 says. The strip's width is a design constant in millimetres with its pattern recorded. Exists today as whole-plane work; the change is scope. Picture: the refined boundary over the acquired plane, the strip visible, silence drawn as silence elsewhere.

### Step 6. The spaces between: the first relations, and the objects inside them
Consumes the boundaries of the big objects and the facing-pair graph. Produces, for each pair of facing surfaces, the interstice: its extent between the two surfaces, its walls read as three layers (cartilage, the dark plate, the marrow behind) by the conjunction digits in the wall strip, its fill by the conjunction class inside, and its contents: each body of tiles or places inside it that is dark on every acquisition becomes a new indexed object, located by the interstice that holds it. This is the second round of the loop: the menisci are the dark bodies at the periphery of the tibiofemoral interstices (the triangles on coronal, the horns on sagittal); the cruciates are the dark bands in the notch interstice on sagittal; the trochlear cartilage faces the patella. Compartments are enumerated by which surfaces face; medial and lateral are named by the fibula witness or abstained. Exists today in part: the relations record is declared and carries sampled pairings; the extent is unbuilt. Picture: each interstice drawn between its two objects, its contents outlined.

### Step 7. The spans and the envelope: the third round
Consumes the objects so far and the leg tiles outside the interstices. Produces: the spans, dark-on-every-acquisition bodies running from one big bone to the other outside the interstice along the medial and lateral exteriors (the MCL on the medial side; the lateral structures as its reference); and the envelope, the fluid-class component connected to the interstices, with its volume, lining and outline, including the recess above the patella and any outpouching posterior to the bones' envelope. Each is located by the objects already resolved and the picture's axes. Picture: spans and envelope over the acquired plane, by index.

### Step 8. The residual, and ownership
Consumes every claim. Produces every place inside the leg owned by one object or named unassigned, and the residual map: what no object claims, with its conjunction classes. The residual is the next structure argument, read again if it holds anything object-shaped. Exists today; the change is that it now runs on a field most of which is already claimed. Picture: the residual.
